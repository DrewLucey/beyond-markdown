# Dementlieu {#rthw:dementlieu:dementlieu}

Domain of Decadent Delusion

> #### **Darklord:** [Saidra d’Honaire](#saidradhonaire) {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire}
>
> **Genres:** [Dark fantasy](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#darkfantasy) and [psychological horror](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#psychologicalhorror)
>

>

>
> **Aligned Tarokka Card:** Charlatan
>
> **Opposed Tarokka Card:** Seer

Composed of the city of Port-a-Lucine and its shining, fog-shrouded outskirts, Dementlieu is a realm where the truth is smothered by collective delusion. Everything in Port-a-Lucine appears to be more than it is, and people behave as if the few examples of grandeur and prosperity were the norm. Beneath its facade, the city is rotten.

Despite pervasive claims of happiness and success, the people of Dementlieu fight to survive and maintain social appearances. Every individual struggles to hide their struggles. Lying is central to life in the domain, creating a breeding ground for con artists and smiling villains.

Behind the domain’s denial of reality is its Darklord, the phantomlike [**Duchess Saidra d’Honaire**](#ref:monster:6491387-saidra-dhonaire "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491387-saidra-dhonaire"). The cynosure of high society, this masked duchess ensnares the city’s upper class in her exaggerated ideas of luxury and civility. During her weekly masquerade balls, Saidra’s guests must perform to her expectations. Those who fail to charm her are decried as impostors and reduced to ash by her magic. Saidra’s terror also grips the city’s desperate. Her murderous, unmasked form—the Red Death—stalks the benighted streets, preying on any who tarnish her preposterous vision of a perfect society.

## Domain Features {#rthw:dementlieu:domainfeatures}

Dementlieu is known for the following features:

**City of Lies.** Nothing in Port-a-Lucine is as it appears. Everyone pretends to be more successful than they are. Lies great and small fill everyday communication. All residents know these truths, but none dares speak of them.

**Grand Masquerade.** Every week [Duchess d’Honaire](#saidradhonaire) holds a Grand Masquerade. Invitations are coveted, and invitees who don’t attend rarely survive for long.

**Lord Governor.** Administration of Port-a-Lucine rests in the hands of Lord Governor Marcel Guignol. He is counseled by aristocratic advisers, including the duchess.

**Masks.** Decorative masks are common accessories, worn at social events and in daily life.

## Locations in Dementlieu {#rthw:dementlieu:locationsindementlieu}

Port-a-Lucine’s grand structures and garish promenades dazzle viewers, distracting most from the grimy streets and cramped homes beyond. The countryside of Chateaufaux is described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the [Dementlieu Locations table](#dementlieulocations).

### Chateaufaux {#rthw:dementlieu:locationsindementlieu:chateaufaux}

The people of Port-a-Lucine speak of the countryside, Chateaufaux, where nobles summer at their estates and where idyllic villages send goods for sale into the city. But no one travels far beyond the city walls, no goods arrive from beyond, and Chateaufaux doesn’t actually exist. The Mists creep close to Port-a-Lucine, occasionally exposing patches of forests and meadows but nothing more. Nevertheless, Chateaufaux remains a convenient explanation for whatever mysteriously appears or disappears.

## Saidra d’Honaire {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire}

Darklord Who Rules Illusions

[![(Artist: Lenka Šimečková)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/06-003.saidra-dhonaire.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/06-003.saidra-dhonaire.jpg)

> “Welcome, treasured guests—you bright jewels in my city’s sterling crown. Bask in all you’ve earned and deserve. Oh… that’s not a loose button, is it?”
>
> —Saidra d’Honaire

Duchess Saidra d’Honaire knows her life isn’t her own—she knows she isn’t alive at all—yet she ignores that for the sparkle of empty celebrations and a parody of her ideal life. The duchess terrorizes Dementlieu, forcing the realm to conform to her desires. Those who expose the flaws in her fantasy and remind her of the desperation she once endured, she slays using her life-sapping power.

All can plainly see Duchess d’Honaire isn’t one of the living. Nevertheless, servants and sycophants flatter her poise, beauty, and array of lavish masquerade costumes. When not hosting decadent social events, Saidra removes her mask and slips into the night. Through the streets and alleys of Port-a-Lucine, the Darklord prowls as the Red Death: a murderous spirit that preys on liars, the struggling, and all who remind her of her humble origins.

Saidra is overbearing and elitist, despite being unworldly. She fears being exposed as a fraud and projects this fear onto others, violently accusing them of being liars on the barest evidence or sign of vulgarity. Yet, in mysterious letters and rumors, Saidra receives evidence that her own simple, spiteful family dwells somewhere beyond her city. Their confessions could bring all her deceptions to light.

#### Traits {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:6491387-saidra-dhonaire:Traits}

**_Darklord Restoration._** If Saidra dies, she revives with all her Hit Points in 1d10 weeks somewhere in Dementlieu.

**_Incorporeal Movement._** Saidra can move through other creatures and objects as if they were [**Difficult Terrain**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#difficultterrain). She takes 5 (1d10) Force damage if she ends her turn inside an object.

**_Lie Detector._** Saidra knows whether she hears a lie.

**_Legendary Resistance (3/Day, or 4/Day in Domain)._** If Saidra fails a saving throw, she can choose to succeed instead.

**_Sunlight Sensitivity._** While in sunlight, Saidra has [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on ability checks and attack rolls.

#### Actions {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:6491387-saidra-dhonaire:Actions}

**_Multiattack._** Saidra makes three Necrotic Burst attacks. She can replace one of these attacks with a use of Spellcasting.

**_Necrotic Burst._** _Melee Attack Roll:_ +7, reach 5 ft. or range 30 ft. _Hit:_ 14 (2d10 + 3) Necrotic damage.

**_Life Drain._** _Constitution Saving Throw:_ DC 15, one creature Saidra can see within 30 feet. _Failure:_ 28 (8d6) Necrotic damage, and its Hit Point maximum decreases by an amount equal to the damage taken. _Success:_ Half damage only.

**_Truth or Die (1/Day)._** Saidra asks a creature she can see within 60 feet to identify itself and casts [**Disintegrate**](#ref:spell:2619094-disintegrate "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619094-disintegrate") targeting the creature, requiring no Material components and using Charisma as the spellcasting ability (DC 15, with [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) if the target lies).

**_Spellcasting._** Saidra casts one of the following spells, requiring no Material components and using Charisma as the spellcasting ability (spell save DC 15):

**At Will:** [**Charm Person**](#ref:spell:2618964-charm-person "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618964-charm-person") (duchess form only), [**Fear**](#ref:spell:2618872-fear "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618872-fear") (Red Death form only).

**_Shape-Shift._** Saidra [**shape-shifts**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#shapeshifting) into the Red Death, or she returns to her true form as the duchess. Her game statistics are the same in each form. Any equipment she is wearing or carrying isn’t transformed.

#### Legendary Actions {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:6491387-saidra-dhonaire:LegendaryActions}

Legendary Action Uses: 3 (4 in Domain). Immediately after another creature’s turn, Saidra can expend a use to take one of the following actions. Saidra regains all expended uses at the start of each of her turns.

**_Lash Out._** Saidra makes one Necrotic Burst attack.

**_Shadow Veil._** Saidra gives herself the [**Invisible**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#invisiblecondition) condition until the start of her next turn and can fly up to half her Fly Speed. The condition ends early immediately after Saidra deals damage. Saidra can’t take this action again until the start of her next turn.

### Saidra’s Tale {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:saidrastale}

Despite being raised in poverty, Saidra d’Honaire was an arrogant bully whose father led her to believe she was secretly royalty. When her father remarried, Saidra suffered slights and mockery at the hands of her callous stepmother and stepsisters.

The night her father revealed that Saidra was only distantly related to nobility, she fled into the night. At her mother’s grave, Saidra’s curses were overheard by a mysterious stranger. This grandmotherly figure offered to grant Saidra any wish. When the stranger heard Saidra’s desire, she cackled and dressed the girl in rubies and a crimson gown. Resplendent, Saidra set off to the masquerade ball celebrating the coronation of the land’s new duke.

At the masquerade, Saidra charmed the duke and his court. Yet, as bells tolled midnight, the gems on her dress evaporated, releasing a magical poison. The evil magic killed everyone at the masquerade, a massacre that would have fulfilled Saidra’s murderous wish and left her within reach of the throne. As Saidra toured a palace full of the dying, clouds of spreading poison rose in a mysterious mist.

When the mist cleared, Saidra found herself in a grand estate in Port-a-Lucine. The estate’s servants acknowledged her as their duchess, but the lethal poison had dissolved Saidra’s body into a deathly vapor. As none seemed to notice, Saidra embraced the fantasy that she had achieved her vain desires.

### Saidra’s Domain {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:saidrasdomain}

Dementlieu is altered by Saidra’s presence, creating the following effects:

**Closing the Borders.** While in her domain, Saidra can close or open the borders to Dementlieu at will. While the borders are closed, the countryside called Chateaufaux appears in the Mists surrounding Dementlieu. Creatures in the Mists get lost amid mazelike hedgerows and fogbanks, only to reappear on the outskirts of Port-a-Lucine 2d6 hours later.

**Empowered Actors.** Humanoids add 1d8 to Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**Performance**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) checks made while wearing a mask.

**Invitation.** At dawn, Saidra can choose a creature whose name she knows or that she’s seen before. A simple mask and an invitation to the next Grand Masquerade appear to that creature. The letter and mask appear in an unobserved but obvious space, such as a bedside table or among gear.

If [Saidra](#saidradhonairestatblock) dies, these effects end immediately. The effects resume if Saidra gains a new body (see her Darklord Restoration trait).

### Saidra’s Lair {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:saidraslair}

The duchess dwells at the d’Honaire Estate. This lavish manor and its manicured grounds cover the largest island in Lucine Bay.

#### D’Honaire Estate {#rthw:dementlieu:saidradhonaire:saidraslair:dhonaireestate}

The d’Honaire Estate is well lit and filled with expensive decor and artwork—some of it quite good, but most is of middling quality. The estate is depicted on [map 2.9](#map209dhonaireestate) and has the following features:

**Grand Entry.** A grand staircase sweeps from the estate’s entry to airy balconies above. This broad space also serves as a ballroom and is the focus of the duchess’s social events.

**Salons.** Elegant rooms with distinct themes fill the estate. Popular spaces include the Skobeloff Salon (themed like a sunken ship), the Sulfur Salon (with dizzying wallpaper), and the Taupe Salon (filled with underwhelming taxidermies).

**Sea Caves and Docks.** Those who travel to the island estate by gondola dock in this sculpted sea cave. Stairs rise from the docks to the main estate.

**Second Floor.** The halls remain locked and guarded. Within are the duchess’s quarters, a joyless, hollow space that the Darklord haunts between stalkings and social events.

## Adventures in Dementlieu {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu}

Adventures set in Dementlieu feature mystery and deception, often involving illusions and fairy-tale grandeur masking depravity and murder. Use this section to develop horror adventures in this domain.

### Horror in Dementlieu {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:horrorindementlieu}

Adventures in Dementlieu often include the following elements.

#### Lies and Liars {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:horrorindementlieu:liesandliars}

In Dementlieu, one can take little at face value. Terrible food might be called gourmet, while horrendous art might be dubbed avant garde. Those who resist such claims might find themselves mocked for not understanding a thing’s appeal. This can lead to challenges that range from the comical to the surreal. Roll on or choose a result from the Dementlieu Deceptions table to inspire overlooked events that might lead to broader consequences.

**Dementlieu Deceptions**

| 1d6 | A Dementlieuse Liar …                                                                        |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Claims to personally know Duchess d’Honaire and so deserve special treatment.                |
| 2   | Insists they’re investing all their resources in solving a problem they’re clearly ignoring. |
| 3   | Is publicly trapped or wounded but claims to passersby that they’re fine.                    |
| 4   | Passes off their rat-meat pies and gutter-water wine as food fads.                           |
| 5   | Poses as an expert, but their advice is ludicrous or dangerous nonsense.                     |
| 6   | Says their stolen wares come from Chateaufaux.                                               |

#### Scourge of the Red Death {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:horrorindementlieu:scourgeofthereddeath}

Between masquerades, Saidra d’Honaire fixates on her past and her fear that people suspect her ignoble origins. At these times, her glamorous facade fades, reducing her to a crimson spirit. In this guise, she slips into the night and scours Port-a-Lucine, targeting the sick, poor, or uncouth; those who disparage nobles; and those she suspects of investigating her past. The resulting life-drained bodies are attributed to a notorious figure locals call the Red Death.

**_Using the Red Death._** While Port-a-Lucine’s residents ignore the Red Death’s victims—potentially chiding corpses for their laziness or public drunkenness—the characters are unlikely to overlook bodies so clearly drained of life. Use such corpses to remind the group that the lies of Dementlieu’s people are dangerous denials of reality.

### Dementlieu Campaign Arc {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:dementlieucampaignarc}

Campaigns set in Dementlieu often involve the discovery and unraveling of a conspiracy. A campaign here might follow this outline:

**Levels 1–3.** Someone asks the characters in Port-a-Lucine for help finding a missing friend, opera performer, or noble. Following the clues, the characters learn this is but one of a rash of disappearances involving dangerous creatures hiding in plain sight—such as [**Gargoyles**](#ref:monster:5195003-gargoyle "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195003-gargoyle"), [**Wererats**](#ref:monster:5195265-wererat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195265-wererat"), or [**Waxworks**](#ref:monster:6491401-waxwork "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491401-waxwork") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#waxwork)).

**Level 4–5.** The mystery points toward an elite member of society. The characters can’t gain access to this individual, but they know the noble attends the Grand Masquerade of Duchess d’Honaire. The characters must prepare for and infiltrate the event (detailed below) to learn more.

**Levels 6–8.** Learning that the suspicious noble supports or benefits from the disappearances, the characters investigate the noble’s home or business (such as the opera house or House of Wax). There, they discover and disrupt the creatures behind the disappearances, likely finding that the vanished people have been turned into monsters. If the noble escapes, an unrefusable invitation to the Grand Masquerade might draw them out, resulting in a showdown during that event—and potentially attracting the ire of [**Duchess d’Honaire**](#ref:monster:6491387-saidra-dhonaire "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491387-saidra-dhonaire").

### The Grand Masquerade {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:thegrandmasquerade}

Adventure for Level 4 Characters

**Situation.** The Grand Masquerade is an opportunity for characters to meet Dementlieuse nobles and gain information they can’t otherwise access.

**Hook.** The date of the next masquerade is public knowledge. To attend, the characters need to create their own costumes and invitations.

[![Map 2.9: D’Honaire Estate View Player Version (Artist: Mike Schley)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/map-06-02.dhonaire-estate.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/map-06-02.dhonaire-estate.jpg)

#### Encounters {#rthw:dementlieu:adventuresindementlieu:thegrandmasquerade:encounters}

The adventure uses map 2.9 and consists of these encounters.

**_Dressed to Kill._** Attendees at the Grand Masquerade are expected to sport lavish costumes featuring fanciful masks. Any character not wearing an appropriate [**Costume**](#ref:equipment:536-costume "https://www.dndbeyond.com/equipment/536-costume"), a mask, and at least 100 GP worth of jewelry (or appropriate magical alternatives) has [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on Charisma checks made during this adventure. Armor can’t be worn with a nonmagical costume. If a character becomes [**Bloodied**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#bloodied) while wearing a nonmagical costume, their costume is destroyed.

**_Arrival._** At the entrance to the estate, servants check the characters’ invitations. A character with a forged invitation must succeed on a DC 10 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check to be admitted. Those who fail are asked to leave. If they refuse, four [**Ghouls**](#ref:monster:5195009-ghoul "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195009-ghoul") wearing formal wear and masks fight them.

**_Masquerade._** The Grand Masquerade unfolds in three tableaux. During each, the characters can decide to dance, gossip, flirt, or do something else. Each character can earn renown for their group with the ball’s attendees and host by making positive impressions on other guests (see [chapter 3](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#renown) of the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024) for details on renown). Each positive interaction can increase the group’s Renown Score by 1 per character per tableau.

Duchess d’Honaire has yet to appear, but hundreds of other costumed nobles mingle during the masquerade. These masked guests might share their names, but a character must succeed on a DC 10 Wisdom ([**Perception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check to identify even someone they’re familiar with. The following guests pay particular attention to the characters:

**Alexandre du Cire** (Medium [**Waxwork**](#ref:monster:6491401-waxwork "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491401-waxwork"); see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#waxwork)) poses as a human and runs the House of Wax. He urges the characters to visit his establishment. His costume is a suit on one side and a ball gown on the other. He is [**Friendly**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#friendlyattitude).

**Angel Pajaro** (Medium [**Werewolf**](#ref:monster:5195267-werewolf "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195267-werewolf")) is a famed diva of the Port-a-Lucine opera and is [**Hostile**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#hostileattitude). She likes to humiliate her fans but loves flattery from other performers. She’s dressed as an arctic fox.

**Julienne d’Honaire** (Medium, Neutral Good [**Bandit**](#ref:monster:5194915-bandit "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194915-bandit")) is a Friendly human. She’s bubbly and behaves in an unrefined manner. She claims to be related to the duchess but confides that she wasn’t invited. Julienne wears a secondhand ballerina costume.

**Lord Governor Marcel Guignol** (Medium [**Noble**](#ref:monster:5195145-noble "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195145-noble")) is [**Indifferent**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#indifferentattitude) and didn’t want to attend the event. The elderly human is jittery and hates loud noises, and he seeks an excuse to leave. He’s dressed as a smiling jester.

**Raoul Rameau** (Medium [**Guard Captain**](#ref:monster:5195064-guard-captain "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195064-guard-captain")) is a member of the city watch. He tries to guess who people are and why they’ve been invited—his hunches are poor. He’s dressed as a toy monkey with cymbals.

**_Uncouth Exchange._** After the second tableau, two revelers wearing the same costumes as two nearby characters spot their lookalikes. Outraged by this faux pas, these [**Berserkers**](#ref:monster:4904621-berserker "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4904621-berserker") fight them, wielding sharpened candelabras (these function as [**Greataxes**](#ref:equipment:21-greataxe "https://www.dndbeyond.com/equipment/21-greataxe")). If the characters defeat their attackers, their Renown Score increases by 2.

**_The Duchess Appears._** After three tableaux, [**Saidra d’Honaire**](#ref:monster:6491387-saidra-dhonaire "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491387-saidra-dhonaire") (see earlier in [this chapter](#saidradhonaire)) appears. She is fabulous and terrible as she drifts over the flattering crowd. Nearing the characters, she halts, gasps in revulsion, and points. “You! Never have I seen such an audaciously incompetent impostor! Who do you think you are to violate these hallowed halls?!” Near a character, Julienne d’Honaire begins to reply, and the duchess instantly disintegrates her. A ghoulish servant soon appears with a dustpan to clean the spot where Julienne stood. If party members spent two or more tableaux with Julienne, the group’s Renown Score decreases by 1.

**_Conclusion._** If the group’s Renown Score is 5 or less, the duchess shrieks, outraged to find more unworthies. Right away, four [**Ghoul**](#ref:monster:5195009-ghoul "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195009-ghoul") servants appear and fight the characters; two **Ghouls** arrive every round until the party flees the estate. The duchess doesn’t fight.

If the characters’ Renown Score is at least 6, the duchess compliments one of their costumes as she drifts by in time with the music. If the characters came to the masquerade to meet or influence someone, that individual appears soon after, impressed by the duchess’s compliment. Other members of the city’s nobility or individuals the characters met earlier in the night also might approach them with invitations or offers of employment. Each character earns a bonus 600 XP.
