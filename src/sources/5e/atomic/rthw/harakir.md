# Har’Akir {#rthw:harakir:harakir}

Domain of the Ancient Dead

Untold generations of tombs and monuments choke on the obsidian sands of the desert domain of Har’Akir. Here, past splendors lie forgotten, and the living bow in service to the mummified god-king, [**Ankhtepot**](#ref:monster:6491339-ankhtepot "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491339-ankhtepot").

From a golden pyramid in the City of the Dead, Ankhtepot reigns as speaker for the gods and immortal pharaoh. The deathless ruler cares nothing for his domain or mortal concerns. Rather, he relentlessly sends servants in search of his one desire: his ka, the missing piece of his fractured soul.

As the pharaoh obsesses over his lost soul, his servants plague the domain in his name. In hidden tomb-courts, mummies known as the Children of Ankhtepot dominate cults as emissaries of mysterious gods. And in the mud-brick city of Muhar, Ankhtepot’s priests oversee all aspects of life, apportioning food to the worthy and punishing blasphemers. But all the pharaoh’s servants also pursue the Darklord’s quest for his mysterious ka, though only his Children know the truth of this “treasure” he seeks. Each of them is desperate for some clue to placate Ankhtepot and spare them from his wrath.

## Domain Features {#rthw:harakir:domainfeatures}

Har’Akir is known for the following features:

**Distant Pharaoh.** The people of Har’Akir know their ruler is an immortal who resides in the golden mountain called Pharaoh’s Rest. Only priests can enter this massive pyramid.

**Extreme Environs.** [Intense heat](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#extremeheat) and [winds](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#strongwind) are common in Har’Akir (see the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024)), and water is scarce.

**Forgotten Ruins.** The deserts of Har’Akir are littered with the remnants of a strange and glorious empire. The origins of these ruins remain a mystery.

**Mysterious Gods.** Har’Akir’s people worship a pantheon of false or silent gods. Manipulative or deluded priests oversee all aspects of society in these gods’ and the pharaoh’s names.

**Preserved Dead.** Akirrans mummify their dead so the pharaoh might call them to service. It is a crime to burn the dead.

## Locations in Har’Akir {#rthw:harakir:locationsinharakir}

Har’Akir is dominated by treacherous, surreal deserts that obey Ankhtepot’s will. The Labyrinth and city of Muhar are described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the [Har’Akir Locations table](#harakirlocations).

### Labyrinth {#rthw:harakir:locationsinharakir:labyrinth}

Har’Akir’s countless trap-laden ruins interconnect beneath the desert in a hidden maze called the Labyrinth. Monstrous [**Mummies**](#ref:monster:5195135-mummy "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195135-mummy") and stranger creatures stalk these sprawling tunnels. Nevertheless, some priests use the few mapped sections to move about the domain unseen.

### Muhar {#rthw:harakir:locationsinharakir:muhar}

The city of Muhar is the only permanent settlement in Har’Akir. Here, pious people thank the gods for the water and food of the Muhar Oasis. From the city’s largest structure, the Temple of Ese, High Priest Isu Rehkotep speaks for Pharaoh Ankhtepot in all matters; her word is considered both royal decree and divine edict. Ankhtepot doesn’t care how Isu runs Muhar so long as she maintains order and uses her influence to continue his unending search. Those who displease Isu or oppose her priest-guards are thrown into the Mouth of Oru, a pit that connects to the Labyrinth.

## Ankhtepot {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot}

Darklord Who Pursues Death

[![(Artist: Anna Podedworna)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/08-003.ankhtepot.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/08-003.ankhtepot.jpg)

> #### “Bow before the pharaoh—whether as a servant or as dust.” {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot:Bowbeforethepharaohwhetherasaservantorasdust}
>
> —Ankhtepot

Ankhtepot has known treachery and conquest. He has known divinity and rule. But now he knows simply boredom and despair. He desires only to recover his lost ka, which he knows lies hidden in Har’Akir. With it, he hopes to become mortal again, die, and face his gods’ judgment. Whether this means peace or oblivion matters not to him. Ankhtepot seeks only an end, and he and his followers will be a plague on Har’Akir until he finds it.

### Ankhtepot’s Tale {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot:ankhtepotstale}

In a nation called the Land of Reeds and Lotuses, Ankhtepot served the royal family as high priest. As old pharaohs died and new ones rose, the influential priest came to believe that the gods wanted a wiser leader to rule. On the day of a new pharaoh’s ascension, Ankhtepot rallied his priests and murdered their liege. But he had misjudged the people’s will, and an outraged mob rose up and executed the traitorous priests.

Moreover, Ankhtepot had misjudged the will of the gods. As he stood before them in death, the immortals forsook him, cursing him and denying him entry to the afterlife. They returned him to the world but stripped away a piece of his soul, his ka—the vital essence that inspires all living beings.

Ankhtepot awoke, trapped within his corpse, as he was mummified along with his treacherous followers. Untold centuries passed, but on the day the last memory of Ankhtepot faded from the Land of Reeds and Lotuses, the high priest’s bindings rotted away, and Ankhtepot crawled forth from his sarcophagus. He emerged from his tomb in an unfamiliar land: Har’Akir.

In this new land, Ankhtepot found a pious people devoted to the same gods he once served. Over generations, he wiped out that religion, replacing it with false divinities of his own imagining for whom he alone could speak. Ankhtepot also resurrected the priests buried alongside him as [**mummy lords**](#ref:monster:5195134-mummy-lord "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195134-mummy-lord"), replacing their heads with those of beasts and monsters holy to his new faith. These Children of Ankhtepot served him as they did in life, and together the dead conquered the souls of Har’Akir.

In the ages since, Ankhtepot hasn’t forged the land of his ambitions. His servants scheme against him, his people don’t flourish, and his monuments crumble to dust in mere centuries. Ankhtepot’s ambitions have withered. Now he seeks only to reclaim his soul and use it to put an end to his accursed immortality.

### Ankhtepot’s Domain {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot:ankhtepotsdomain}

Har’Akir is altered by Ankhtepot’s presence, creating the following effects:

**Blistering Sun.** Any outdoor area of [**Bright Light**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#brightlight) in Har’Akir is an area of [extreme heat](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#extremeheat) (see the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024)).

**Closing the Borders.** While in his domain, Ankhtepot can close or open the borders to Har’Akir at will. While the borders are closed, whirling sands fill the Mists surrounding Har’Akir. A creature that enters the Mists for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there takes 2d6 Slashing damage.

**Control Undead.** Undead of CR 5 or lower in Har’Akir have the [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) condition and obey Ankhtepot’s orders.

If Ankhtepot dies, these effects end immediately.

### Ankhtepot’s Lair {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot:ankhtepotslair}

A mountain-size black pyramid, Ankhtepot’s tomb—called Pharaoh’s Rest—looms over the City of the Dead. It can be seen from all across Har’Akir.

#### Pharaoh’s Rest Features {#rthw:harakir:ankhtepot:ankhtepotslair:pharaohsrestfeatures}

Ancient and ornate, Pharaoh’s Rest holds countless carvings of mysterious deities and Ankhtepot as an idealized pharaoh. A labyrinth of trapped tombs, embalming rooms, and treasure chambers fills the majority of this massive pyramid. Broad paths used by Ankhtepot’s servants lead past deadly side passages to the heights of the monument. Prominent locations are depicted on [map 2.14](#map214pharaohsrest) and have the following features:

**Ankhtepot’s Tomb.** The burial treasures and reliefs lining Ankhtepot’s tomb’s walls are shattered, having suffered ages of Ankhtepot’s frustration. Hidden behind a bejeweled throne is a thoroughly defaced sarcophagus. Within, Ankhtepot awaits word of his ka’s discovery, rising only when disturbed by trespassers or at the call of servants from the funeral barge chamber below.

**Funeral Barge.** Ankhtepot envisions his trip to the afterlife taking place on a river. In an ornate ritual site, he has prepared a funeral barge and other ceremonial trappings necessary for his journey after his ka’s recovery.

**Lesser Tombs.** Throughout the pyramid are shrines, mummification chambers, and the crypts of Ankhtepot’s servants. These include the burial chambers of several Children of Ankhtepot and the Darklord’s son, Thutepot.

**Shrine of Ankhtepot.** A vast, open shrine holds ominous statues of Har’Akir’s gods and columns etched with exaggerated and fictitious events from Ankhtepot’s rule. On the night of the full moon, a hidden magical elevator activates, granting access to the tombs above.

#### Children of Ankhtepot {#rthw:harakir:adventuresinharakir:horrorinharakir:childrenofankhtepot}

Ankhtepot resurrected the priests who died alongside him as [**Mummy Lords**](#ref:monster:5195134-mummy-lord "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195134-mummy-lord"), granting each the head of an animal or monster corresponding to the fictitious gods he imposed on Har’Akir. The Children of Ankhtepot style themselves as harbingers of these gods while they manipulate cults or the domain’s priesthood. Some have grown tired of Ankhtepot’s detachment and seek to depose the pharaoh—particularly the treacherous mummy lord Senmet.

### Har’Akir Campaign Arc {#rthw:harakir:adventuresinharakir:harakircampaignarc}

Campaigns set in Har’Akir often involve the domain’s treacherous priests, its ancient tombs, and the hunt for Ankhtepot’s ka. A campaign here might follow this outline:

**Levels 1–4.** The characters help a priest named Basras search a trap-laden, buried palace complex for a mysterious treasure. Within, they discover a set of oversize canopic jars. They take them to Isu Rehkotep in Muhar, but the high priest deems them worthless. Basras is thrown into the Mouth of Oru.

**Levels 5–10.** Isu Rehkotep hires the characters to search for the pharaoh’s missing treasure. She sends them to Senmet’s Tomb to recover an ark full of ancient scrolls, claiming these are what the pharaoh desires. (She doesn’t actually know the object of the pharaoh’s search.)

**Levels 11–15.** After the characters return the scrolls to Isu, Basras (now an Undead), reveals that the high priest seeks to use them to control a [**Mummy Lord**](#ref:monster:5195134-mummy-lord "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195134-mummy-lord") and oppose Ankhtepot, as detailed in the “Scrolls of the Dead” adventure.

### Scrolls of the Dead {#rthw:harakir:adventuresinharakir:scrollsofthedead}

Adventure for Level 12 Characters

**Situation.** High Priest Isu Rehkotep claims to have found the treasure Ankhtepot has been searching for and prepares to offer it to him during a ritual at the pyramid of Pharaoh’s Rest. In truth, she plans to use a scroll that controls the Child of Ankhtepot called Senmet to destroy the pharaoh and claim his throne.

**Hook.** The priest De’rah fears the pharaoh will vent his wrath on the people of Muhar when the high priest is discovered. She offers the characters 5,000 GP and a random magic item from the [Relics—Rare table](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/random-magic-items#relicsrare) in the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024) if they stop Isu.

**_Ritual Site._** On the funeral barge level of the pyramid, Isu Rehkotep (Medium, Lawful Evil [**Archpriest**](#ref:monster:5194903-archpriest "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194903-archpriest")) readies to call forth Ankhtepot from his tomb using a ritual involving a gong on the ceremonial ship. Meanwhile, two [**Priests**](#ref:monster:5195170-priest "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195170-priest") and two [**Death Cultists**](#ref:monster:5194960-death-cultist "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194960-death-cultist") defend her. Isu avoids battle, but if any of her allies fall, she produces an ancient papyrus scroll and reads it, summoning Senmet.

**_The Mummy._** If Isu reads her scroll, 1 round after her allies are defeated, the [**Mummy Lord**](#ref:monster:5195134-mummy-lord "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195134-mummy-lord") Senmet appears. Isu orders him to destroy the characters, and he obeys. Senmet fights until he or the scroll the high priest holds is destroyed. Isu doesn’t join the battle and attempts to escape if Senmet is destroyed.

**_The Scroll._** A character adjacent to Isu can steal the scroll from her by succeeding on a DC 16 Dexterity ([**Sleight of Hand**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check or by taking an action if she has the [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition) or [**Restrained**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#restrainedcondition) condition. Any character can take an action to destroy the scroll but can’t use it to command Senmet. If it is destroyed, Senmet turns [**Hostile**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#hostileattitude) toward Isu.

**_The Pharaoh._** Once Isu and Senmet are dealt with, [**Ankhtepot**](#ref:monster:6491339-ankhtepot "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491339-ankhtepot") (see earlier in [this chapter](#ankhtepot)) appears at the path leading up to his tomb. He demands the characters depart immediately but return with his missing ka. If they do so, fabulous riches will be theirs. If they don’t, they will know his wrath. Characters who escape the tomb after the encounter with Ankhtepot earn a bonus 4,700 XP.
