# Hazlan {#rthw:hazlan:hazlan}

Domain Doomed by Magic

> #### **Darklord:** [Hazlik](#hazlik) {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik}
>

Hazlan is a domain drained of vitality, frayed by magical disasters, and overrun with abominations. Here the amoral, spellcasting elite have so exploited magic that it’s turned erratic and dangerous to use.

Encouraging these magical abuses is [**Hazlik**](#ref:monster:6491361-hazlik "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491361-hazlik"), the domain’s zulkir, a title meaning wizard-overlord. The Darklord postures as the domain’s most powerful wizard and views all others as either apprentices or test subjects. All the while, Hazlik hides the fact that he’s lost the ability to learn new magic. Paranoid and controlling, Hazlik constantly spies on those in his domain through the Eyes of Hazlik. These pervasive, eye-shaped symbols appear on structures, decorations, clothing, and individuals across the domain—and through these eyes, the Darklord watches. Hazlik surveils his domain endlessly, reveling in the fear of his people, encouraging rivalries between his apprentices, and searching for magical innovations he can claim as his own.

## Domain Features {#rthw:hazlan:domainfeatures}

Hazlan is known for the following features:

**Eye of Hazlik.** Inhabitants claim the eyelike design called the Eye of Hazlik bears Hazlik’s blessing and wards off dangerous magic. In truth, the symbol is a sensor for Hazlik’s magical surveillance.

**Magocracy.** The wizard [Hazlik](#hazlik) rules Hazlan. His apprentices have free rein to exploit the land and its people to further their magical experiments.

**Shattered by Magic.** The effects of magical disasters disfigure the domain, poisoning rivers with alchemical runoff, scarring the land with magical blasts, and unleashing unpredictable hazards.

**Wild Magic.** Magic is unreliable in Hazlan, resulting in dangerous side effects (see “[Hazlan Wild Magic](#hazlanwildmagic)”).

## Locations in Hazlan {#rthw:hazlan:locationsinhazlan}

Magical disasters and supernatural environments make Hazlan’s arid landscape even deadlier and its natural rock monuments even stranger. The settlements of Sly-Var and Toyalis are described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the [Hazlan Locations table](#hazlanlocations).

### Sly-Var {#rthw:hazlan:locationsinhazlan:slyvar}

In Sly-Var, mages obsessed with earning the Darklord’s favor raise architecturally discordant towers that defy physical laws. The residents magically create whatever they need, resulting in little need for trade. This leaves the labyrinthine streets and knotted bridges between intertwined compounds eerily empty. The few common folk who traverse these surreal streets often disappear—the victims of magical guardians or of wizards who see the magically deficient as stock for their experiments.

### Toyalis {#rthw:hazlan:locationsinhazlan:toyalis}

The largest town in Hazlan, Toyalis boasts a population of poor, hardworking commoners united by terror of the domain’s magic-using elite. Colorful depictions of magical creatures, arcane symbols, and the Eye of Hazlik cover the town’s rough wooden and adobe structures. Every few weeks, one of Hazlik’s apprentices leads a procession of attendants and magical wonders to Toyalis in search of replacement servants, new apprentices, or stock for magical experiments. A carnival atmosphere ensues, with the apprentice displaying their magical accomplishments and setting up competitions to assess the locals’ capabilities—magical or otherwise. When a mage’s procession leaves, it takes townsfolk deemed valuable with it.

## Hazlik {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik}

Darklord of Desperation and Magical Disaster

[![(Artist: Marianne Khalil)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/09-003.hazlik.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/09-003.hazlik.jpg)

> “You’re new, I can tell. Impress me, and maybe I’ll let you be my newest apprentice.”
>
> —Hazlik

The paranoid, egomaniacal archmage Hazlik clings to his power despite his magical ambitions being supernaturally smothered. Since Hazlik entered Hazlan, his magic has become unstable. Spells he casts behave erratically and fade swiftly—most lasting no more than an hour—while his magical creations fall apart in about a day. The idea of others learning this terrifies the Darklord, which leads him to claim others’ works as his own, spy on potential rivals, and order reckless research into the nature of magic and his domain.

Hazlik suffers vivid nightmares wherein past rivals taunt and torment him. The Darklord avoids sleeping, employing magical tinctures to stay awake, but this causes him to vacillate between bouts of exhaustion, jitters, and paranoia.

Hazlik knows that his domain is being ripped apart by magical disasters—many of his own creation. He openly denies the realm’s devastation, though, his fear of cascading ruin clashing with his inability to admit he’s wrong.

### Hazlik’s Tale {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik:hazlikstale}

On the world of Toril, Hazlik rose among the ranks of the merciless Red Wizards of Thay. Arrogant and convinced mortality was a weakness, he sought hidden truths of magic, ways to visit realms within the psyche, and command of reality.

As Hazlik’s power increased, he developed rivalries with other Red Wizards, many of which ended with his foes magically scarred. But one rival Hazlik couldn’t defeat: the wizard Indreficus. Hazlik gradually came to obsess over Indreficus, viewing him as his only worthy opponent. Indreficus shared Hazlik’s genius and arrogance, and they spent seasons as vicious adversaries and passionate lovers. They drove one another to feats of magical brilliance both revolutionary and unspeakable. This culminated when Hazlik discovered that Indreficus planned to betray Hazlik to garner the favor of Thay’s ruling zulkirs. Heartbroken and enraged, Hazlik captured Indreficus and subjected him to nightmarish magical manipulations that permanently transformed him into a pain-wracked, living magical portal. Hazlik presented his creation to the zulkirs, hoping to gain their patronage

The zulkirs revealed that Indreficus hadn’t betrayed Hazlik. Rather, the ruling mages had watched the pair’s ascent and, wary of their ambitions, undermined them before they became threats. Moreover, they deemed Hazlik’s transformation of Indreficus an abomination. As punishment, the zulkirs decreed that every mage Hazlik had scarred could do the same in return to him, marking him with their personal symbols. As the first brand-wielding member of the mob of Hazlik’s rivals closed on the wizard, Hazlik fled through the groaning portal that was Indreficus.

Hazlik emerged amid the pain-wracked dreamscapes of Indreficus. Unwittingly, Hazlik had entered the Domain of Dread known as the [Nightmare Lands](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/other-domains-of-dread#nightmarelands), where Indreficus’s shrieking and condemnation hounded him. After months of torment, Hazlik escaped into the Mists and eventually emerged into a realm that knew nothing of magic. His Thayan tattoos had turned gold and warped into symbols meaning “traitor.” Undaunted, Hazlik used his magic to dominate the people of this new realm, claiming the title of zulkir and dubbing his realm Hazlan.

### Hazlik’s Domain {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik:hazliksdomain}

Hazlan is altered by Hazlik’s presence, creating the following effects:

**Closing the Borders.** While in his domain, Hazlik can close or open the borders to Hazlan at will. While the borders are closed, the Mists surrounding Hazlan are filled with monstrous magical experiments. A creature that enters the Mists for the first time on a turn or starts its turn there must succeed on a DC 15 Dexterity saving throw or take 5 (1d10) Force damage and have the [**Restrained**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#restrainedcondition) condition until the start of its next turn. A creature makes this save only once per turn.

**Frayed Magic.** Whenever a creature (excluding Hazlik and his allies) casts a spell using a level 5+ spell slot, it rolls on the [Wild Magic Surge table](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/character-classes-continued#wildmagicsurge) in the [Player’s Handbook](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024) to create a magical effect.

**Magic Nightmares.** While in his lair, Veneficus, Hazlik can cast [**Clairvoyance**](#ref:spell:2618972-clairvoyance "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618972-clairvoyance"), requiring no spell components and using the same spellcasting ability as his Spellcasting action. When the spell is cast this way, its range is all of Hazlan, Hazlik can see but can’t hear through the sensor, and the sensor can appear only on an Eye of Hazlik.

If [Hazlik](#hazlikstatblock) dies, these effects end immediately. The effects resume if Hazlik gains a new body (see his Darklord Restoration trait).

### Hazlik’s Lair {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik:hazlikslair}

Hazlik’s crimson-towered palace, Veneficus, stands atop the jagged mesa called the Red Rise. The Darklord is attended by a staff of magical creations and his most accomplished apprentices.

#### Veneficus Features {#rthw:hazlan:hazlik:hazlikslair:veneficusfeatures}

Veneficus is littered with frivolous magic effects and magic items that no longer keep Hazlik’s attention. The site is depicted on [map 2.16](#map216veneficus) and has the following features:

**Halls of Triumph.** Multiple audience chambers, display galleries, and pleasure gardens rival one another in decadence, each exhibiting grotesque artwork and trophies of Hazlik’s achievements.

**House of Solitude.** The secluded House of Solitude is magically locked, and Hazlik forbids anyone from entering. At night, muffled screams and flickering lights emanate from within.

**Red Tower.** Hazlik’s personal chambers fill the Red Tower. Magical portals connect the untold numbers of demiplanar chambers that make up the tower. Somewhere within lies Hazlik’s orrery, a multistory magical device that allows the Darklord to see through any Eye of Hazlik in the domain.

## Adventures in Hazlan {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan}

Tales in Hazlan involve radical magical experiments, supernatural environments, rivalries between mages, and magical surveillance. Use this section to develop horror adventures in this domain.

### Horror in Hazlan {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:horrorinhazlan}

Adventures set in Hazlan often include the following elements.

#### Eyes of Hazlik {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:horrorinhazlan:eyesofhazlik}

Hazlik spies on his domain’s inhabitants using the Eye of Hazlik. This eyelike sigil adorns buildings, clothing, talismans, and creatures across the domain. Locals claim the symbol protects them from dangerous magic and creatures. Hazlik uses the eyes to spy on useful or threatening mages and to reinforce his reputation as an ever-present overlord.

Hazlik often sends loyal apprentices or magical creatures to capture those whose behavior witnessed through the eyes offended him. He turns such individuals into fodder for experimentation in Sly-Var or Veneficus.

#### Hazlan Wild Magic {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:horrorinhazlan:hazlanwildmagic}

As a result of unchecked arcane experimentation, magic functions erratically in Hazlan. Whenever a creature in Hazlan expends a spell slot to cast a spell or activates a magic item, it rolls 1d10. On a 1, it unleashes a random magical effect. Roll on the [Wild Magic Surge table](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/character-classes-continued#wildmagicsurge) in chapter 3 of the [Player’s Handbook](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024) to determine the effect. Consider describing these results in unsettling ways that don’t change their effects, as in these examples:

- Summoned creatures use the proscribed stat blocks but are cosmetically warped to resemble undead or aberrant creatures.
- Effects that teleport the caster relocate the caster into a misty expanse full of threatening shapes.
- Effects on creatures cause unsettling sensations, such as the feeling of earwigs wriggling beneath one’s skin or one’s teeth struggling free.

Anyone who openly wears an Eye of Hazlik can choose not to roll on the Wild Magic Surge table in this way once per day.

#### Hazlik’s Apprentices {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:horrorinhazlan:hazliksapprentices}

Dozens of mages serve as Hazlik’s apprentices. Between infrequent, demeaning group lessons with the Darklord, these apprentices pit themselves against one another to impress Hazlik by pushing the boundaries of magical innovation. Those who gain Hazlik’s favor are promised arcane secrets known only to the Darklord—secrets that might not exist. This creates a cadre of Hazlani mages who recklessly seek to impress Hazlik by the following means, among others:

- Changing the nature of reality to suit their whims
- Creating monuments in Hazlik’s honor
- Devising new magic items, spells, or supernatural materials
- Forging unique magical servants for Hazlik
- Stealing rare magic items
- Unleashing dramatic displays of magical might

Hazlik’s current favorite apprentices include the masked wizard Yhal the Skygazer, the shadow-cloaked Ruzelo, and the daring genius Eleni.

### Hazlan Campaign Arc {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:hazlancampaignarc}

Campaigns set in Hazlan often involve mages abusing their power to garner Hazlik’s fickle favor. A campaign here might follow this outline:

**Levels 1–3.** The characters arrive in Toyalis, where servants of the mage Khrinitov abduct locals with supposed psychic potential. Eleni, an apprentice of Hazlik, believes Khrinitov’s experiments are pointless. She hires the characters to rescue the villagers from Khrinitov’s tower amid the Orbitoclasts. In doing so, the characters reveal Khrinitov to be an [**Intellect Devourer**](#ref:monster:5195088-intellect-devourer "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195088-intellect-devourer"), a [**Brain in a Jar**](#ref:monster:6491344-brain-in-a-jar "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491344-brain-in-a-jar") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#braininajar)), or another aberrant being.

**Levels 4–8.** Eleni reveals that she’s trying to undermine Hazlik and protect the people of Hazlan. She tells the characters of a magic mineral in the Mound of the Worm that [**Purple Worms**](#ref:monster:5195174-purple-worm "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195174-purple-worm") pursue. She encourages the party to collect the mineral and use the purple worms to destroy locations like the Marks. As the characters do so, they face magical guardians and hazards. During their travel, they’re exposed to strange magic and might gain Dark Gift feats (see [chapter 1](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/character-options#darkgifts)).

**Levels 9–12.** Eleni asks the characters to come to Sly-Var to plan a raid on Hazlik’s palace. There, she reveals that she’s been exposing them to dangerous magic from across the domain. She entraps them with a portal that leads to arena-like laboratories connected to Veneficus, where [**Hazlik**](#ref:monster:6491361-hazlik "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491361-hazlik") pits them against terrors his other apprentices have created. The characters must escape, potentially facing Eleni and Hazlik along the way.

### Eyes of the Darklord {#rthw:hazlan:adventuresinhazlan:eyesofthedarklord}

Adventure for Level 10 Characters

**Situation.** Subjects of magical experimentation seek to escape Hazlan, but every time they try, they’re spotted through the Eyes of Hazlik, and the Darklord’s apprentices hunt them down.

**Hook.** Allies hire the characters to help them escape Hazlan by destroying Hazlik’s orrery in Veneficus. They offer to pay the party 4,000 GP once they’re guided to another domain.

**_Flawed Magic._** Among mundane decorations, this room contains a random magic item. Roll on the [Arcana—Common table](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/random-magic-items#arcanacommon) in chapter 7 of the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024). The item functions once, causes a wild magic surge (see “[Hazlan Wild Magic](#hazlanwildmagic)”), and then crumbles to dust.

**_Red Tower._** Shifting portals connect the passages in the Red Tower. Every time the characters pass through a portal in the tower, roll 1d8. If the number rolled is lower than the number of portals passed through, the characters arrive in the orrery chamber. On an 8, the characters appear at the entrance to Veneficus.

**_Orrery Chamber._** This room is dominated by a magical orrery only Hazlik can use. It is defended by a [**Relentless Juggernaut**](#ref:monster:6491383-relentless-juggernaut "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491383-relentless-juggernaut") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#relentlessjuggernaut)). The orrery has AC 19, HP 25, and Immunity to Psychic damage. Every time the orrery takes damage, roll on the [Wild Magic Surge table](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024/character-classes-continued#wildmagicsurge) in chapter 3 of the [Player’s Handbook](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/phb-2024). Negative effects affect the character who damaged the orrery, while beneficial effects affect the juggernaut.

**_Hazlik._** After the orrery is destroyed, [**Hazlik**](#ref:monster:6491361-hazlik "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491361-hazlik") (see earlier in [this chapter](#hazlik)) appears and offers a magic-using character power and influence as his apprentice. If the character accepts, he commands that character to murder the others. If the character declines, Hazlik mocks the party. The Darklord uses magic to flee if he takes any damage, swearing revenge. Characters who destroy the orrery and escape Veneficus earn a bonus 3,100 XP.
