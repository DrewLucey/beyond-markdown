In Mordent, the dead don’t rest. Here, farmers and fishers toil in scattered villages, cleaving to stoic, hardworking traditions. In the manors of landed, aristocratic families, the bearers of noble names view themselves as conservators of the land and of stifling reputations. Tradition, memory, and expectation haunt the lives of Mordent’s people, and when they die, nothing changes.

Little known to the living, the dead in Mordent rise as spirits, and for most—knowing no other ways—their work continues. Abandoned estates hide spectral generations of frustrated nobles and their downtrodden servants. While some spirits drift idly or deteriorate to mere impressions of their past selves, others greedily cling to their former possessions and ambitions. Some pernicious spirits become infamous hauntings, while others—such as the domain’s Darklord, the ghostly [**Lord Wilfred Godefroy**](#ref:monster:6491403-wilfred-godefroy "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491403-wilfred-godefroy")—seek to dominate the living and dead.

## Domain Features {#rthw:mordent:domainfeatures}

Mordent is known for the following features:

**Lingering Spirits.** All manner of spectral undead and other hauntings lurk in Mordent. Ghost stories and rumors of haunted sites are common.

**Local Nobility.** Mordent is governed by town mayors and local lords. Few nobles remain, and some have only decrepit manors left to their names.

**Old Faiths.** People casually worship vague deities in Mordent, but evidence of forgotten druidic traditions lies scattered around the wilds.

**Spiritualism.** Attempting to communicate with the dead is a popular pursuit for investigators, bored nobles, and charlatans.

**Tradition and Shame.** The people of Mordent follow traditions that prioritize hard work, self-sufficiency, and avoiding public embarrassment. Locals overlook blatant strangeness so not to shame their neighbors, and every family has secrets.

## Locations in Mordent {#rthw:mordent:locationsinmordent}

Mordent is a foggy land of forests, fields, and moors that endures frequent rainstorms. The land is dotted with tiny farming hamlets and the rotting homes of forgotten nobles.

The town of Mordentshire is detailed below, and other notable locations are summarized on the [Mordent Locations table](#mordentlocations).

### Mordentshire {#rthw:mordent:locationsinmordent:mordentshire}

Mordentshire is the largest community in Mordent. Sturdy fisherfolk work the town docks and, after dusk, fill crowded tavern common rooms. Cold winds frequently blow in from Arden Bay. When they stop, they’re replaced by a shroud of fog the locals call “the breath of the dead.”

To the chagrin of the town’s no-nonsense populace, Mordentshire is home to a small community of scholars and adventurers. Saulbridge Sanitarium provides a refuge for the ill while also welcoming researchers from other domains. Across town, an unassuming herbalist shop is run by [**Rudolph van Richten**](#ref:monster:6491386-rudolph-van-richten "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491386-rudolph-van-richten") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#rudolphvanrichten)), an investigator of all things monstrous and mysterious.

The lands around Mordentshire host two prominent estates. Heather House is the home of the Weathermay family, regional lords who count numerous heroes among their number. To the east stands Gryphon Manor, the notorious House on Gryphon Hill. The mansion has a history of violence, tragedy, and hauntings and is the home of the domain’s Darklord, Lord Wilfred Godefroy.

## Wilfred Godefroy {#rthw:mordent:wilfredgodefroy}

Darklord Tormented by Ghostly Undeath

[![(Artist: Winona Nelson)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/14-003.wilfred-godefory.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/14-003.wilfred-godefory.jpg)

> “Speak your piece, then hold your tongue. No fools are more insufferable than the living.”
>
> —Wilfred Godefroy

Lord Wilfred Godefroy rules the afterlife of all who die in Mordent. Arrogant, impatient, and vengeful, the Darklord seeks any opportunity to exert his control and reinforce his undying noble standing.

Lord Godefroy haunts the House on Gryphon Hill, along with countless other spirits. Wilfred can send mobs of obedient spirits to do his will anywhere in the domain. He uses his control over the dead to influence the living, threatening to harm his victims’ dead loved ones if they don’t obey his fickle commands.

A generation ago, the creators of the mist-piercing device called the [Apparatus](#theapparatus) vanished from Mordent. Lord Godefroy has discovered the hidden machine, but, to his frustration, lacks a way to use it.

After 1 minute, it succeeds automatically.

**_Bone-Chilling Step._** Wilfred teleports up to 60 feet to an unoccupied space he can see, and each creature within 10 feet of the space he left takes 4 (1d8) Necrotic damage. Wilfred can’t take this action again until the start of his next turn.

### Wilfred’s Tale {#rthw:mordent:wilfredgodefroy:wilfredstale}

In life, Lord Wilfred Godefroy was an unremarkable minor aristocrat. He inherited the estate of Gryphon Hill near Mordentshire centuries ago by murdering his father and framing it as an accident. During a bloody night years later, the entire Godefroy family—Wilfred; his wife, Estelle; and their daughter, Penelope—died violently. But instead of passing on to the afterlife, the Godefroys’ souls lingered at Gryphon Hill. In death, Wilfred is haunted by the ghosts of his family, recent and the past generations, who blame him for their deaths and the family’s disgrace.

A generation ago, a mysterious alchemist created a device in Mordent called the [Apparatus](#theapparatus). This magical machine had many fantastic powers, including the ability to open passages between the planes and separate a creature’s good and evil natures into separate beings. The Apparatus drew multiple powerful beings to Mordent and eventually attracted the Mists themselves when the device malfunctioned, causing a deadly disaster. The Mists collected Mordent into the Domains of Dread, and the mysterious alchemist vanished. With the departure of mightier beings, Lord Godefroy remained as the land’s most fearful villain. He inherited the mantle of Darklord of the new domain of Mordent.

Since then, Wilfred has claimed lordship of all who die in Mordent. He knows the location of the Apparatus and seeks to master its powers, but how to do so and the location of a vital piece of the machine—the Rod of Rastinon—both elude him. He seeks to blackmail anyone who might be able to reveal the device’s secrets or find the missing rod. Wilfred forces Alice Weathermay, mayor of Mordentshire, to keep him appraised of useful visitors to the domain, by holding her dead husband, Daniel Foxgrove, hostage.

### Wilfred’s Domain {#rthw:mordent:wilfredgodefroy:wilfredsdomain}

Mordent is altered by Lord Godefroy’s presence, creating the following effects:

**Closing the Borders.** While in his domain, Lord Godefroy can close or open the borders to Mordent. While the borders are closed, the groans and whispers of the dead fill the Mists surrounding Mordent. Creatures in the Mists have the [**Deafened**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#deafenedcondition) condition.

**Ears Everywhere.** While in his domain, Lord Godefroy can cast [**Sending**](#ref:spell:2619015-sending "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619015-sending"), requiring no Material components and using Charisma as the spellcasting ability. When casting the spell in this way, Lord Godefroy can target any Undead creature in Mordent, in addition to the usual targets.

**Spectral Serfs.** A Humanoid that dies in Mordent rises 7 days later as a [**Specter**](#ref:monster:5195210-specter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195210-specter") under Lord Godefroy’s control, unless the Humanoid is restored to life.

If Wilfred dies, these effects end immediately.

### Wilfred’s Lair {#rthw:mordent:wilfredgodefroy:wilfredslair}

The House on Gryphon Hill lies just outside Mordentshire and is home to Lord Wilfred Godefroy. Hundreds of spirits remain bound to the house and the surrounding grounds, and all serve the lord of the house and struggle to stay in his good graces. Spirits that rebel are punished—either forced to face Wilfred’s kennel of spectral hounds or dragged into the deepest recesses of the house, which even the eldest spirits fear.

#### House on Gryphon Hill {#rthw:mordent:wilfredgodefroy:wilfredslair:houseongryphonhill}

Cobwebs and dust coat the House on Gryphon Hill. Lurking spirits remain out of sight until trespassers pose a threat or Lord Godefroy orders them to attack. The house has the following features:

**Basement.** Lord Godefroy orders ghostly teams to endlessly dig deeper and wider basements beneath the house. As if creating his own underworld, Wilfred imprisons spirits who displease him in the expanding haunted dungeons beneath the house. Somewhere amid these dungeons, a passage connects to the site where the [Apparatus](#theapparatus) is hidden.

**Haunted Rooms.** Estelle and Penelope Godefroy haunt the house’s attic, avoiding Wilfred and doing all they can to frustrate the Darklord and stymie his ambitions. The house’s other spirits obey Estelle and Penelope unless their commands contradict an order from Wilfred.

**Withered Estate.** Wilfred’s ghostly hounds patrol the house’s grounds, while spectral groundskeepers tend to dead topiaries and hedges as if they still thrived.

## Adventures in Mordent {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent}

Mordent is a realm of ghost stories (see [chapter 4](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#ghoststories)). Moldering manors, lonely moors, neglected graveyards, and similar gothic sites provide natural settings for encounters with spirits and the tragic dead. Use this section to develop horror adventures in this domain.

### Horror in Mordent {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:horrorinmordent}

Adventures in Mordent often include the following elements.

#### The Apparatus {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:horrorinmordent:theapparatus}

Wilfred is obsessed with control over the dead and the living. He knows where the magical device known as the Apparatus is hidden but has limited understanding of how to use it. Nevertheless, he knows it is powerful and can produce devastating effects. He cares nothing for the machine’s remarkable abilities and seeks to use it as a weapon to force all in Mordent to obey him.

To fulfill his goals, Wilfred seeks individuals with magical and alchemical insights, as well as rumors of the Apparatus’s missing piece, the Rod of Rastinon. He might threaten or blackmail the living into aiding him. Should he suspect the Rod of Rastinon or other vital information lies beyond Mordent, he might send his agents to other domains.

The Apparatus can exchange souls, open passages through the Mists, and create more dangerous effects, but the magical device might have any properties an adventure demands.

#### Bastion of Heroes {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:horrorinmordent:bastionofheroes}

Despite the horrors lurking in Mordent, the domain hosts several quiet, relatively peaceful communities. Characters from Mordent might have had relatively peaceful, mundane lives before mysteries or tragedies led them to adventure. The abandoned estates of Mordent also make fine places to use the rules for Haunted Bastions, detailed in [chapter 3](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/ravenloft-adventures#hauntedbastions).

#### Ghosts of Mordent {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:horrorinmordent:ghostsofmordent}

Ghost stories often involve spirits that linger as the result of some tragedy or unfinished work. In Mordent, spirits linger and might request help from the living. Roll on or choose a result from the Mordent Hauntings table to inspire adventures featuring the lingering dead.

**Mordent Hauntings**

| 1d8 | The Spirit Seeks Aid In …                                                              |
| --- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Freeing a living loved one from possession by another ghost.                           |
| 2   | Hiding a secret that could embarrass or undermine a living loved one.                  |
| 3   | Liberating another spirit from the dungeons beneath the House on Gryphon Hill.         |
| 4   | Locating a spirit that wanders a broad region or who appears only during the new moon. |
| 5   | Restoring the memory of an ancient spirit that has almost lost its sense of self.      |
| 6   | Searching a distant haunted site for a spirit it has been separated from.              |
| 7   | Sealing the site of an ancient evil or the lair of a creature that torments spirits.   |
| 8   | Smuggling a living relative out of Mordent, away from Wilfred’s clutches.              |

Even though spirits are trapped in Mordent, many find secluded places where they reflect on nature, their lives, and eternity. Over generations, such spirits often become little more than vague shapes or disembodied voices or emotions before fading away entirely.

#### Van Richten’s Herbalist Shop {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:horrorinmordent:vanrichtensherbalistshop}

[**Rudolph van Richten**](#ref:monster:6491386-rudolph-van-richten "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491386-rudolph-van-richten") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#rudolphvanrichten)) is the foremost scholar of monsters and strange occurrences in the Domains of Dread. He lives and works from the Mordentshire herbalist shop (see [map 2.27](#map227vanrichtensherbalistshop)). Beyond the storefront, Rudolph’s studies and prepares methods of combating evils plaguing the Land of the Mists. Adventurers might find lore and tools to combat all manner of monsters here.

When absent from his shop, Rudolph leaves it in the care of his protégés, [**Gennifer**](#ref:monster:6491356-gennifer-weathermay-foxgrove "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491356-gennifer-weathermay-foxgrove") and [**Laurie Weathermay-Foxgrove**](#ref:monster:6491368-laurie-weathermay-foxgrove "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491368-laurie-weathermay-foxgrove") (detailed in [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#weathermayfoxgrovetwins)) and Beatrice Polk, an elderly local gardener and accomplished detective.

### Mordent Campaign Arc {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:mordentcampaignarc}

Mordent campaigns often involve ghostly mysteries. A campaign here might follow this outline:

**Levels 1–2.** Caarey Gelthik, a local ne’er-do-well in Mordent, has become a [**Ghast**](#ref:monster:5195007-ghast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195007-ghast") and lurks beneath the town docks. By night, he robs graves to consume the bodies within. The characters take note when silent spirits—perhaps of allies—begin wandering town, each missing limbs, tongues, and other soft features. Only finding and defeating Gelthik causes the dismember spirits to depart.

**Levels 3–4.** Beatrice Polk, caretaker at the Mordentshire herbalist shop, hires the characters to end a haunting afflicting the shop. See the adventure “[The Ghost Hunter’s Haunting](#theghosthuntershaunting).”

**Levels 5–10.** Alice Weathermay, mayor of Mordentshire, hires the characters to find a lost piece of local history: the Rod of Rastinon. She shares leads involving various strange sites, but the rod doesn’t appear. Eventually, [**Lord Godefroy**](#ref:monster:6491403-wilfred-godefroy "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491403-wilfred-godefroy")—who has been working through Alice Weathermay—grows impatient. He threatens the party’s dead loved ones, insisting the characters find the rod swiftly and help him use the Apparatus (see [chapter 3](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/ravenloft-adventures)).

### The Ghost Hunter’s Haunting {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:theghosthuntershaunting}

Adventure for Level 3 Characters

**Situation.** An accident at [Rudolph van Richten’s](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#rudolphvanrichten) herbalist shop broke a strange relic and unleashed a deadly feline spirit from another domain.

**Hook.** Beatrice Polk, the shop’s caretaker, offers the characters 400 GP if they end the haunting.

[![Map 2.27: Van Richten’s Herbalist Shop View Player Version (Artist: Sean Macdonald)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/map-14-03.van-richtens-herbalist-shop.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/map-14-03.van-richtens-herbalist-shop.jpg)

#### Encounters {#rthw:mordent:adventuresinmordent:theghosthuntershaunting:encounters}

This adventure uses map 2.27 and consists of these encounters.

**_Beatrice Polk’s Aid._** Beyond her folksy politeness and dislike of curse words, Beatrice Polk takes her stewardship of the herbalist shop seriously but doesn’t enter the shop during the haunting. She can provide the characters with a number of [**Potions of Healing**](#ref:magic_item:8960641-potion-of-healing "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/8960641-potion-of-healing") equal to the party’s members. She scuttles plans that involve harming or stealing Rudolph’s possessions.

**_Ghost Cat._** A [**Wraith**](#ref:monster:5174960-wraith "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5174960-wraith") that appears as a black cat the size of a panther haunts the shop. Whenever the characters enter a room without monsters present, roll a die. If the number is odd, the cat is in the room. If the cat has more than half its Hit Points, it fights for a round, then flees to another room or floor; otherwise, it flees without attacking. If destroyed, the wraith reappears 24 hours later with full Hit Points. Its threat ends only if the ritual in the cellar is completed.

**_Laboratory._** Two [**Poltergeists**](#ref:monster:5195168-poltergeist "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195168-poltergeist") haunt this room.

**_Stairs._** An unnatural wind blows against anyone who tries to ascend either stairway to the second floor. A character must succeed on a DC 16 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check to climb the stairs.

**_Dumbwaiter._** A dumbwaiter connects the kitchen and the upstairs hall. It can hold one Medium or smaller creature. A creature outside the dumbwaiter can use an action to raise or lower the dumbwaiter and its contents.

**_Upstairs Hall._** Broken on the floor is a strange relic: a mummied cat. Shelves nearby hold accounts of Rudolph’s travels in a land called [Har’Akir](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/harakir).

**_Library._** Rudolph’s library is filled with occult texts. Any character who spends a minute investigating the library finds a copy of _Van Richten’s Guide to Ghosts._ Those who spend at least 10 minutes reading this book and have seen both the relic in the upstairs hall and the ritual room in the cellar understand how to use the cellar room to exorcise the ghostly cat. Additionally, a character who succeeds on a DC 12 Intelligence ([**Investigation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check finds a [**Spell Scroll**](#ref:magic_item:8960643-spell-scroll-level-1 "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/8960643-spell-scroll-level-1") of [**Protection from Evil and Good**](#ref:spell:2618912-protection-from-evil-and-good "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618912-protection-from-evil-and-good").

**_Guest Room._** Standing facing a corner of this room is [**Gennifer Weathermay-Foxgrove**](#ref:monster:6491356-gennifer-weathermay-foxgrove "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491356-gennifer-weathermay-foxgrove") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#genniferweathermayfoxgrove)). She is possessed by a [**Ghost**](#ref:monster:5195008-ghost "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195008-ghost"), which holds her in a catatonic state. Gennifer is freed of her possession if she’s affected by a spell like _Protection from Evil and Good_ or if she’s removed from the shop. The feline-featured wraith attacks the party if its possession ends or if Gennifer is moved from the room. If freed from the ghost’s control, Gennifer is aware of the ghostly cat, as she accidentally broke the relic in the hall. She knows how to exorcise the spirit using the wards in the cellar.

**_Cellar._** A room in the cellar is prepared for magic rituals. A character who succeeds on a DC 12 Intelligence ([**Arcana**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check realizes this space is warded to contain or dispel spirits. If the characters place the relic from the upstairs hall within the space, the ghostly cat appears 1 minute later and fights until destroyed. If the cat is defeated here, the relic repairs itself and the haunting ends. Each character gains a bonus 400 XP for ending the haunting.
