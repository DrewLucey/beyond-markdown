Terrors haunt the Mists. This chapter begins with advice to help you, the DM, use monsters to create frightful encounters. Following this is a collection of horrifying monsters and stalwart monster hunters for your adventures in the Domains of Dread.

## Horror Monsters {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters}

Cultivating mystery and menace around the monstrous foes in your adventures can be a simple matter. Here are techniques you can use to transform any monster into a horror to haunt characters’ nightmares.

### Monstrous Origins {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:monstrousorigins}

Unique monsters are singularly terrifying. Consider presenting the monster central to your adventures as a one-of-a-kind terror. Such a creature might be a fluke of nature, the product of insidious magic, a purposefully terrifying creation of the Dark Powers, or a being with an even more unsettling background. Use the tips for creating Darklords and drawing inspiration from different genres of horror in [chapter 4](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#genresofhorror) to create unique origins for the most important threats in your adventures.

Different origin stories suggest diverse paths for adventurers to follow when investigating these creatures, learning their weaknesses, and ensuring nothing like them ever menaces again.

### Terrifying Language {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:terrifyinglanguage}

Describe monsters in vivid ways. A terror chasing characters could just “follow” them, but instead it might “wriggle,” “surge,” or “galumph” after them. Descriptive language underscores how a creature behaves and conjures a visceral description of the monster. Unexpected descriptions can emphasize a creature’s bizarre nature and unsettle the players. For example, it’s one thing to say a snake “slithers,” but another to say a sinister shadow or cruel witch does.

Use unsettling descriptions and similes. For example, a vine blight’s constricting vines might resemble “noose-like cords” or “oily digits.” Try less literal descriptions—even nonsensical ones. For example, a flesh golem might howl like “an earthquake in a mortuary” or “a charnel pit that’s learned to sob.”

### Notorious Monsters {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:notoriousmonsters}

Treat creatures central to your horror adventures as infamous threats. The more you foreshadow their menace or reveal that they have threatened others before, the larger such creatures loom. Build dread by giving monsters reputations that suggest the monsters’ form, deeds, or peculiarities while letting players’ imaginations embellish details.

For example, tales might describe a skeletal horror that whistles cheerfully while committing brutal acts. Characters might hear rumors of the Whistling Fiend’s melodies becoming haunting earworms for survivors of the fiend’s attacks. A party seeking the monster might hear it whistling in the Mists long before they confront it. All that time, characters can speculate about their enemy’s nature, but ultimately the Whistling Fiend might be any threat you choose.

### Mysterious Monsters {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:mysteriousmonsters}

Use conflicting descriptions and strange behaviors to cultivate mystery around your monster. The less characters know about a creature, the more they’ll speculate about terrifying possibilities.

You can develop uncertainty about a creature even after it appears. Keep players guessing about an adventure’s threat by referring to a monster by a flavorful description rather than the name of its stat block. A “zombie” doesn’t sound like much of a threat, but a “stumbling mass of fetid skin and shattered bones” might be something else entirely.

### Weird Traits {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:weirdtraits}

By adding unexpected traits to monster stat blocks, you can undermine players’ expectations of what a monster is and what it can do. Consider how you can use the [Creature Traits](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#creaturetraits) list in the “[Creating a Creature](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#creatingacreature)” section of the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024) to give familiar monsters horrifying twists.

### Putting It All Together {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:horrormonsters:puttingitalltogether}

Combine the techniques in this section to create a unique terror. If you have ideas about what you want your monster to do, write them down. Then develop stories that connect those pieces or reinforce the type of horror you want to create.

[![A zombie beholder’s terror is transformed when the monster is recast as a flesh-stitched horror (Artist: Helge C. Balzer)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/22-002.zombie-beholder.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/22-002.zombie-beholder.png)

For example, perhaps you’ve got an idea for an arrogant mage who sought to prove he didn’t need his adventuring companions’ protection by constructing a suit of living armor. Considering the bizarre, magical whims of such a character, you choose a [**Zombie Beholder**](#ref:monster:5194922-beholder-zombie "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194922-beholder-zombie") as the foundation for the mage’s animate armor. To reinforce its nature as a magical creation, you change the monster’s creature type from Undead to Construct and describe it as being made of stitched-together animal parts. Then you give it the Berserk and Magic Resistance traits of a [**Flesh Golem**](#ref:monster:5194997-flesh-golem "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194997-flesh-golem"). The characters’ surprise at unexpectedly facing a beholder might be startling enough, but you add to your story that the mage has become trapped within his living armor as it hunts his former companions. Only by defeating the horror might the characters free what remains of the mage within, represented by the Emissary of Juiblex creature trait from the “[Creating a Creature](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox#creatingacreature)” section of the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024).

### Bodytaker Plants {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:bodytakerplants}

Body-Snatching Alien Flora

**Habitat:** Forest, Grassland, Hill, Swamp; **Treasure:** Any

Bodytaker plants are intelligent organisms that originated on a long-ruined planet of the Material Plane. To outlive their doomed world, these enigmatic plants spread their seeds to the stars. In rare cases, their meteorite-like seeds fall to fertile worlds and take root. What emerges is a new bodytaker plant that seeks to grow and propagate until its kind dominates the entire world. As assistants, the plant creates eerie vegetative duplicates of the world’s inhabitants called bodytaker podlings. Podlings serve the bodytaker plants’ will, infiltrating societies and replacing the individuals they resemble. Ultimately, podlings seek to isolate inhabitants so they can become fertilizer for the bodytaker plant that created them or fodder for more podlings.

#### Bodytaker Podling {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:bodytakerplants:bodytakerpodling}

A bodytaker podling is a near-perfect replica of a creature consumed by a bodytaker plant. A podling possesses the appearance and memories of the being it duplicates, but it is entirely loyal to the bodytaker plant that created it. Fundamentally, a podling isn’t its own creature but an expendable appendage of a bodytaker plant.

Bodytaker podlings frequently exhibit behaviors that suggest their true nature. Most often, they miss the nuances of social interactions. Some might talk in a stilted, jerky cadence or refer to themselves in the third person. Close friends and family members of those replaced by a podling might realize that their loved one has changed, but few imagine the cause behind such a dramatic shift. A podling might try to avoid suspicion by claiming to be ill or redirect doubt by insisting others are behaving oddly. If confronted with evidence of its plant nature, or if caught tending to a bodytaker plant, the podling throws itself into battle and fights to the death.

Roll on or choose a result from the Podling Behaviors table to inspire the unusual habits a podling might demonstrate.

#### Bodytaker Plant {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:bodytakerplants:bodytakerplant}

A bodytaker plant uses its powerful vines to capture victims or accept captives brought to it by podling servants. The plant drags prey into an attached pod where the captive is dissolved over the course of hours. After the process is complete, a duplicate of the captive emerges as a subservient podling. Nothing of the original creature remains, though its noncompostable gear might be recovered after the bodytaker plant is slain.

A bodytaker plant’s podlings are extensions of the plant’s will, and the plant has no qualms about sacrificing podlings or using them as shields in battle. While bodytaker plants can communicate telepathically, most do so only in desperate cases, preferring to speak through their podlings—potentially speaking through several at once.

A bodytaker plant grows where its seed fell from space, or it might later have its podlings relocate it to a hidden sanctuary. Roll on or choose a result from the Bodytaker Plant Sanctuary table to inspire where such a plant might grow.

A creature outside the pod and within 5 feet of the plant can take an action to make a DC 14 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check. On a successful check, the target is removed from the pod, falls in a space within 5 feet of the plant, and has the [**Prone**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#pronecondition) condition. If the plant dies, the trapped target is no longer Blinded or Paralyzed and can escape from the pod using 5 feet of movement, exiting Prone.

#### Reactions {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:bodytakerplants:Reactions}

**_Transfer Harm._** _Trigger:_ An attack roll hits the plant while a podling is within 5 feet of the plant. _Response:_ The plant halves the damage it takes from that attack, and the podling takes the other half of the damage.

### Boneless {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:boneless}

Flayed Flesh with Murderous Intent

**Habitat:** Any; **Treasure:** None

Boneless are the animate skins of crushed or flensed humanoids. They rise from the corpses of those who suffered brutal ends such as flaying, crushing, or cannibalism. These hateful skins might seek vengeance, constricting and smothering victims using their elastic bodies. Alternatively, necromancers might create boneless to serve as stealthy assassins.

Vile magic-users might order a boneless to hang like a grotesque banner or adorn a skeleton or zombie. When foes approach, the boneless peels away from its surroundings or undead ally to face them.

Centuries ago, Osybus, a mysterious figure of immense ambition and evil, founded the order of priests that now bears his name. Osybus sought to use others’ souls to achieve immortality. He forged pacts with any entity that promised him more power, and he delved into any eldritch secret that could possibly prolong his life. He became a devotee of the Dark Powers and tapped into their immortal malice to fuel his apotheosis. As his power grew, he shared his secrets with disciples who wished to defy death in exchange for their worship.

Osybus achieved his goal and became a lich of almost godly power. He rewarded his disciples with a trace of his power in the form of shadowy tattoos. Osybus’s boon gave his priests the ability to steal souls and to cheat death by transforming into undead horrors.

The threat posed by Osybus and his disciples raised alarms far and wide. The Ulmist Inquisition—which at the time counted then-mortal Count Strahd von Zarovich as an ally—faced Osybus in battle. The priests of Osybus still seek to unleash Strahd from the Mists, often using adventurers as pawns. They continue to bear their hated founder’s name, for they know it is his original gift that gives them their horrific powers.

By the time Rudolph tracked down his missing son, the curse had already taken hold: Erasmus had been transformed into Metus’s vampire spawn. Rudolph put an end to his son’s suffering by pounding a wooden stake through the youth’s chest.

Rudolph knows that his age or vocation will eventually put an end to his endeavors. Increasingly he turns his research toward [Barovia](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/barovia) and its vampire overlord, [**Strahd von Zarovich**](#ref:monster:6491390-strahd-von-zarovich "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491390-strahd-von-zarovich"), believing they hold keys to undoing myriad evils across the Land of the Mists.

[![(Artist: Hinchel Or)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/22-043.zombies.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/22-043.zombies.png)

The zombies of Ravenloft come in many monstrous forms. These Undead horrors befoul and beleaguer any living thing in their way.

#### Swarm of Zombie Limbs {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:swarmofzombielimbs}

Dismemberment sometimes isn’t enough to disable a zombie’s undying rage. Swarms of zombie limbs reanimate from the slough of zombie flesh molters and surge on hapless monster hunters who mistakenly believed their work is done.

#### Traits {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:Traits}

**_Swarm._** The swarm can occupy another creature’s space and vice versa, and it can move through any opening large enough for a Tiny chunk of flesh. The swarm can’t regain Hit Points or gain [**Temporary Hit Points**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#temporaryhitpoints).

#### Actions {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:Actions}

**_Rotten Flesh._** _Melee Attack Roll:_ +4, reach 5 ft. _Hit:_ 9 (2d6 + 2) Necrotic damage, or 5 (1d6 + 2) Necrotic damage if the swarm is [**Bloodied**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#bloodied), and the target has the [**Poisoned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#poisonedcondition) condition until the end of its next turn.

**CR** 4 (XP 1,100; PB +2)

#### Traits {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:Traits}

**_Undead Fortitude._** If damage reduces the zombie to 0 Hit Points, it makes a Constitution saving throw (DC 5 plus the damage taken) unless the damage is Radiant or from a Critical Hit. On a successful save, the zombie drops to 1 Hit Point instead.

**_Viral Aura._** _Constitution Saving Throw:_ DC 12, any creature that starts its turn in a 10-foot [**Emanation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#emanationareaofeffect) originating from the zombie. _Failure:_ The target has the [**Poisoned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#poisonedcondition) condition until the start of its next turn. While Poisoned, the target can’t regain Hit Points. _Success:_ The creature is immune to this zombie’s Viral Aura for 24 hours.

#### Actions {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:Actions}

**_Multiattack._** The zombie makes two Slam attacks.

**_Slam._** _Melee Attack Roll:_ +5, reach 5 ft. _Hit:_ 6 (1d6 + 3) Bludgeoning damage plus 9 (2d8) Necrotic damage.

**_Virulent Miasma (1/Day)._** _Constitution Saving Throw:_ DC 12, each creature in a 30-foot [**Emanation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#emanationareaofeffect) originating from the zombie. _Failure:_ 14 (4d6) Poison damage. _Success:_ Half damage. _Failure or Success:_ If this damage reduces the target to 0 Hit Points, it dies and rises 1 minute later as a [**Zombie**](#ref:monster:4775851-zombie "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775851-zombie").

#### Actions {#rthw:denizens-of-the-mists:bestiary:zombies:Actions}

**_Multiattack._** The zombie makes two Slam attacks.

**_Slam._** _Melee Attack Roll:_ +8, reach 10 ft. _Hit:_ 15 (3d6 + 5) Bludgeoning damage.

**_Flesh Entomb (Recharge 5–6)._** _Strength Saving Throw:_ DC 16, one creature the zombie can see within 30 feet. _Failure:_ 16 (3d10) Bludgeoning damage. If the target is a Large or smaller creature, it is entombed in a pile of dead flesh. While entombed, the target has the [**Restrained**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#restrainedcondition) condition, has [**Total Cover**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#cover) against attacks and other effects outside the dead flesh, and takes 10 (3d6) Necrotic damage at the start of each of its turns.

The target can be freed if the dead flesh is destroyed (AC 10; HP 25; Immunity to Necrotic, Poison, and Psychic damage).
