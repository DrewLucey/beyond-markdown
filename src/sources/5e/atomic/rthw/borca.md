# Borca {#rthw:borca:borca}

Domain of Desire and Deceit

> #### **Darklords:** [Ivana Boritsi](#ivanaboritsi) and [Ivan Dilisnya](#ivandilisnya) {#rthw:borca:ivanaboritsi)}
>
> **Genres:** [Gothic horror](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#gothichorror) and [psychological horror](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/creating-domains-of-dread#psychologicalhorror)

[![(Artist: Pam Wishbow)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-001.beggar-tarokka.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-001.beggar-tarokka.png) [![](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-002.tempter-tarokka.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-002.tempter-tarokka.png)

The noble families of Borca engage in an endless war of conspiracies and lethal betrayals as they pursue influence, thrills, and age-old rivalries. People here value guile and callousness, and none embody those traits better than the domain’s two Darklords: the genius poisoner [**Ivana Boritsi**](#ref:monster:6491366-ivana-boritsi "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491366-ivana-boritsi") and the childishly cruel stalker [**Ivan Dilisnya**](#ref:monster:6491365-ivan-dilisnya "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491365-ivan-dilisnya").

Beyond the fashionable holdings of Borca’s elite, commoners struggle against crime, poverty, and starvation. In scattered villages and tenement-filled cities, locals view nobles as celebrities, and the commoners’ idealized vision of noble life leads them to mimic the aristocrats’ callousness and appetite for empty fads. Desperate commoners are tempted into debt and depravity in pursuit of nobles’ favor and passing fancies. Those who don’t bend to the whims of Borca’s elite face humiliation before they’re crushed.

## Domain Features {#rthw:borca:domainfeatures}

Borca is known for the following features:

**Two Darklords.** [Ivana Boritsi](#ivanaboritsi) and [Ivan Dilisnya](#ivandilisnya) are both Darklords of Borca. They control the domain equally—one of countless facts that contributes to their mutual loathing.

**Cruel Intentions.** Borca’s most influential families are vicious rivals bound by blood, politics, and business. Their rivalries unfold in an endless shadow war of lies, shifting alliances, and assassinations. The nobles value appearances above all else; they favor stylish poisons, clever clockwork devices, and public embarrassment in their plots.

**Desperate Sycophants.** Commoners own little, renting homes in crowded cities or rural villages that serve noble interests. Many commoners desperately court the favor of the elite.

**Noble Intrigue.** Nobles control all aspects of Borcan society. They squabble over artistic, political, and religious influence to feed their gluttonous egos and fill their idle lives. The Boritsi family holds the greatest influence.

## Locations in Borca {#rthw:borca:locationsinborca}

The impoverished lands of Borca languish in the shadow of opulent noble estates and garish monuments to greed. The exploited land bears the scars of age-old rivalries and the hiding places of noble secrets.

The capital of Levkarest is described below, and other notable locations are summarized on the [Borca Locations table](#borcalocations).

### Levkarest {#rthw:borca:locationsinborca:levkarest}

Borca’s capital of Levkarest stands at the pinnacle of culture and ostentatious power. Here, lesser nobles pursue fickle dramas and vogue trends while the populace labors amid workhouses and cultlike fashion houses. Commoners glimpse this world of toxic glamor only from afar or when nobles exploit street-level fads.

At the city’s heart rises the People’s Parliament, the land’s ineffective seat of government. Here, unpopular members of noble families squabble over meaningless legislation. The nation’s true rulers conduct business on noble estates, in avant-garde clubs, and in the offices of faceless mercantile ventures.

Near the People’s Parliament stands the Great Cathedral. Within, pious commoners and ostentatious aristocrats attend ceremonies sacred to the deity Ezra. Locals say the cathedral was created by nobles who believed they’d earned the ire of some great evil. Centuries later, these families still support the church, transactionally investing in dubious supernatural insurance.

## Ivana Boritsi {#rthw:borca:ivanaboritsi}

Darklord of Poisonous Ambition

[![(Artist: WeirdUndead (Karina Refrynn))](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-003.ivana-boritsi.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-003.ivana-boritsi.jpg)

> “Your kind can’t possibly fathom how much this sort of interruption costs the House of Boritsi. Don’t make it worse by being tedious.”
>
> —Ivana Boritsi

Ivana Boritsi tires of playing games no one realizes she’s already won. The head of Borca’s most influential family, Ivana effortlessly manipulates all aspects of society. Her servants, employees, and fractious cousins prove endlessly unreliable, forcing her to attend personally to tedious duties and social functions to maintain her power.

Ivana looks eternally youthful, causing some to doubt her competence and attribute her success to her elder cousin, [**Ivan Dilisnya**](#ref:monster:6491365-ivan-dilisnya "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491365-ivan-dilisnya"). Perpetually bored with and disappointed by predictable people, she takes refuge in her alchemical studies and her gardens of poisonous plants. Her vast knowledge of toxins makes her a peerless poisoner. She uses her creations to make people more pliable and to assassinate those she deems too boorish to live. Secretly, Ivana seeks someone who can match her intellect and relieve her boredom, but she quickly grows weary of even the most charming and clever souls.

### Ivana’s Tale {#rthw:borca:ivanaboritsi:ivanastale}

The firstborn of a fantastically wealthy family, Ivana Boritsi was clever, poised, and—in the eyes of her father, Klaus—utterly unfit to lead the family. Driven to prove her father wrong and encouraged by her mother, Camille, Ivana learned every aspect of her family’s vast perfume and medicine business.

Her focus on her goals faltered when she met Pieter, a skilled chemist in her family’s service. Camille recognized her daughter’s budding romance and saw the lowborn Pieter as a threat to the girl’s prospects. Camille seduced Pieter and arranged for her daughter to discover them, intent on convincing her to forsake this and all future romances.

Ivana took a different lesson from the experience. Realizing the depths of her family’s depravity, she poisoned her mother and her callous younger siblings with toxins of her own design. None suspected Ivana, but soon after, Klaus summoned her. Having lost his sons, Klaus named her cousin, Ivan Dilisnya, his heir. Ivana was the last to be informed, her father having already announced his decision to the estate and sent a messenger to inform the Dilisnyas.

Having expected this, Ivana had poisoned the perfumery on the estate’s grounds, turning its far-ranging smells deadly. Ivana laughed as toxic mist poured across the estate. As she sipped the antidote, she watched Klaus and the manor’s staff die choking amid a violet fog. When the poison cleared, the Mists had surrounded the land of Borca (for details on Ivana’s [domain](#ivanaandivansdomain) and [lair](#ivanaslair), see “[Ivan Dilisnya](#ivandilisnya)”).

## Ivan Dilisnya {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya}

Darklord of Childish Cruelty

[![(Artist: WeirdUndead (Karina Refrynn))](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-004.ivan-dilisnya.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/rthw/ogMxaVTj4GqgnUqr/04-004.ivan-dilisnya.jpg)

> “Oh ho oh! What succulent insipidity I see in those lumpish eyes! What divine folly! I swoon, I croon, I know! You’ll be a new favorite!”
>
> —Ivan Dilisnya

Ivan Dilisnya—a spoiled, selfish attention seeker—has the tastes of a child but a withered wreck of a body. Borcan society widely regards this recluse as an innovator and a genius. In truth, he is neither. Ivan is a wicked and off-putting manipulator who, despite his appearance and intellect, affects childlike behaviors and speech patterns for attention. He is alone—despite having been born with everything—and the solitude terrifies him.

By staying out of sight or using deceptive correspondence, Ivan makes his targets doubt reality and overestimate his control. He manipulates people into performing pointless errands for him or spying on his cousin, [**Ivana Boritsi**](#ref:monster:6491366-ivana-boritsi "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491366-ivana-boritsi"), whom he believes mocks him and has caused his aged, squalid state.

Ivan surrounds himself with dangerous clockwork toys and fawning, mechanical servants. While he goes through the motions of crafting such devices, the Dark Powers provide him with any creation he desires, no matter how bizarre.

### Ivan’s Tale {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanstale}

Treachery is tradition for the Dilisnya family. The land of Borca originates from the same world as Barovia’s conquerors, the von Zaroviches. Dilisnyas were at Castle Ravenloft the night of Strahd von Zarovich’s transformation.

Ages later, the siblings Boris and Camille Dilisnya plotted to steal the prestige of their cousins, the Boritsis. Camille manipulated her way into the Boritsi family, while Boris prepared his son, Ivan, to act as head of a united Boritsi–Dilisnya house.

Despite abundant opportunities, Ivan resented his family’s plans for him and grew to embody puerile vice and impulses. His family’s wealth and doting servants exacerbated this problem, even as Ivan fixated on childish toys and behaviors. Frequent “accidents” transpired around him, forcing his family to cover up the maimings and deaths of numerous pets and servants. Only his beloved sister, Kristina, remained immune to his wicked tricks. Although Ivan’s family grew unsettled by Ivan, they sheltered the future head of their house, reshaping their estate and investing in whimsical diversions for him.

On the same night Ivana Boritsi poisoned her family, Ivan learned his parents intended to send Kristina to a prestigious boarding school. Screams, cruel cackling, and clockwork screeching filled the estate that night. When it ended, Ivan Dilisnya was the last living member of his family, and the Mists had closed around Borca.

### Ivana and Ivan’s Domain {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanaandivansdomain}

Borca is altered by Ivana’s and Ivan’s presences, creating the following effects:

**Closing the Borders.** While in their domain, Ivana or Ivan can close or open the borders to Borca at will. While the borders are closed, the Mists surrounding Borca are filled with briar vines (Ivana) or barbed wire (Ivan). The Mists become [**Difficult Terrain**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#difficultterrain), and whenever a creature moves into or within the Mists, it takes 2d4 Piercing damage for every 5 feet it travels. Once Ivana or Ivan closes or opens the borders, neither Darklord can do so again until the next dawn.

**Ivana’s Whispers (Ivana Only).** Ivana can encode a message of 25 words or fewer into flowers in Borca at dawn. Until the next dawn, the first time a creature smells a flower, it hears the message in its mind, understanding the meaning of the message.

**Mystery Letters (Ivan Only).** Whenever Ivan writes a letter and addresses it to a creature in Borca whose name he knows and that he’s seen before, the letter appears near that creature at the next dawn. The letter appears in an unobserved but obvious space, such as a bedside table or among gear.

**Spies.** While in Borca, Ivana and Ivan can cast [**Clairvoyance**](#ref:spell:2618972-clairvoyance "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618972-clairvoyance"), requiring no Material components and using Intelligence as the spellcasting ability. To cast the spell, there must be a Large or bigger plant (Ivana) or a Tiny doll or similar toy (Ivan) within range from which the sensor appears.

If a Darklord of Borca dies, that Darklord’s effects end immediately. Those effects resume if that Darklord gains a new body (see [Ivana’s](#ivanaboritsistatblock) and [Ivan’s](#ivandilisnyastatblock) Darklord Restoration traits).

### Ivana’s Lair {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanaslair}

The vast Boritsi estate, Misericordia, hosts fields, greenhouses, and alchemy labs used to create Boritsi-branded perfumes and tinctures. Within the manor, the Darklord cultivates rare and unique plants in greenhouse-like conservatory wings only she can enter.

#### Misericordia Conservatories {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanaslair:misericordiaconservatories}

The conservatories of Misericordia, the Boritsi estate, hold strange plants, many of which Ivana cultivated. One such site is depicted on map 2.4 and has the following features:

**Gardens.** Plants of exceptional beauty and toxicity flourish under Ivana’s care in Misericordia’s gardens and greenhouses. Many require unusual fertilizers to flourish—particularly the carnivorous plants.

**Laboratory.** In her lab, the Darklord brews deadly alchemical concoctions and wondrous perfumes. A sizable perfume organ here is filled with rare scents and lethal poisons.

**Nursery.** Hidden behind a rotating wall is Ivana’s private nursery and study. Here she cultivates new plant species, many of which have supernatural properties or monstrous life.

### Ivan’s Lair {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanslair}

The Dilisnya estate, Degravo, is a confusing tangle of topiary gardens, hedge mazes, and neglected petting zoos that abut sheer cliffs. At the heart of the grounds sits a largely abandoned compound that includes the Laughing House, Ivan’s home.

#### The Laughing House {#rthw:borca:ivandilisnya:ivanslair:thelaughinghouse}

The only occupied structure in Degravo, the Laughing House has been renovated and redecorated to suit a child’s whimsical tastes. The site is depicted on map 2.4 and has the following features:

**Ivan’s Playroom.** On the second floor of the manor, a gallery of childish art leads to doors minded by a clockwork attendant that Ivan can taunt intruders through. At the hall’s end is a fake bedroom decorated for a neglected youngster. A hidden door there leads to Ivan’s true personal chambers. This playroom is a wonderland of ingenious toys, all of which are secretly deadly. Beyond the Darklord’s personal chamber lie secret rooms where he creates new playthings and discards toys he’s grown tired of.

**Playhouse.** Beyond a lobby attended by clockwork staff, a theater hosts brutal stage productions for Ivan’s amusement. Performers might be life-size toys or desperate prisoners.

#### Nobles of Borca {#rthw:borca:adventuresinborca:horrorinborca:noblesofborca}

Twelve depraved families rule over every aspect of life in Borca. Each is a core family of fifteen or fewer irredeemable individuals united around the head of the house, who tyrannically controls the clan’s myriad ventures. Servants and business representatives across the domain support these core families, as do members of branch families—lackeys with different surnames who are distantly related to the core family or indentured to them.

#### Supernatural Intrigue {#rthw:borca:adventuresinborca:horrorinborca:supernaturalintrigue}

Magic and the supernatural heighten the gilded depravities of Borca. When planning adventures in Borca, consider including the following elements.

**_Curses and Poison._** [Chapter 3](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024/dms-toolbox) of the [Dungeon Master’s Guide](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/dmg-2024) describes curses, magical contagions, and poison. Villains in Borca might use these or adjusted versions of your own design—perhaps with signature cosmetic effects.

**_Monsters._** Monsters can serve as threats but also agents of noble houses. [**Shape-shifting**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#shapeshifting) creatures such as [**Imps**](#ref:monster:4775825-imp "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775825-imp") and [**Wererats**](#ref:monster:5195265-wererat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195265-wererat") excel at infiltration, while [**Doppelgangers**](#ref:monster:5174955-doppelganger "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5174955-doppelganger") and [**Succubi**](#ref:monster:4904865-succubus "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4904865-succubus") might pose as nobles, innocents, or trusted allies. Creatures like [**Ghosts**](#ref:monster:5195008-ghost "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195008-ghost") and [**Intellect Devourers**](#ref:monster:5195088-intellect-devourer "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195088-intellect-devourer") might control individuals, making them behave in unusual ways. Choose a single monster, and consider how you might make it central to a devious noble’s plot.

**_Spells and Magic Items._** Don’t try to account for or incorporate all magic into adventures involving supernatural intrigue. Choose a single spell—such as [**Clairvoyance**](#ref:spell:2618972-clairvoyance "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618972-clairvoyance"), [**Geas**](#ref:spell:2618940-geas "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618940-geas"), or [**Speak with Dead**](#ref:spell:2619068-speak-with-dead "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619068-speak-with-dead")—and consider how to make it central to a plot involving secrets and mysterious revelations. Consider doing the same with a magic item such as a [**Cloak of the Bat**](#ref:magic_item:4608-cloak-of-the-bat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/4608-cloak-of-the-bat"), a [**Hag Eye**](#ref:magic_item:9228705-hag-eye "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/9228705-hag-eye"), or [**Sending Stones**](#ref:magic_item:9180147-sending-stones "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/9180147-sending-stones"). Characters should earn rewards for using their own magic in clever ways.

### Borca Campaign Arc {#rthw:borca:adventuresinborca:borcacampaignarc}

Campaigns set in Borca typically involve the domain’s noble families. A campaign here might follow this outline:

**Levels 1–3.** A scheming noble targets the characters, presenting them with an opportunity or exorbitant compensation for a simple task. In the aftermath, the characters discover they have given up something of great value or realize they look like criminals.

**Levels 4–6.** The scheming noble or a rival offers the characters a way out of their predicament, but this involves a dangerous confrontation or daring infiltration. Supernatural forces—such as jilted doppelgangers, cursed family members, or dead nobility—might aid the characters. Against expectations, the characters complete their mission and turn the tables on their foe.

**Levels 7–8.** The characters’ activities attract the attention of [**Ivana Boritsi**](#ref:monster:6491366-ivana-boritsi "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491366-ivana-boritsi") or [**Ivan Dilisnya**](#ref:monster:6491365-ivan-dilisnya "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491365-ivan-dilisnya"). The Darklord invites the characters to their estate or corresponds with them from afar, using them as agents in their rivalry. For example, either Darklord might task the characters with seeking out the missing will of Ivana’s father, Klaus.

### Lethal Lavender {#rthw:borca:adventuresinborca:lethallavender}

Adventure for Level 5 Characters

**Situation.** The human siblings Eroso and Siobhan Manduchi (Medium, Neutral Evil [**Nobles**](#ref:monster:5195145-noble "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195145-noble")) are scions of a minor noble family who stole a rare plant, not realizing it belonged to the Boritsis. After discovering the owner, they feign their deaths to avoid reprisal.

**Hook.** Eroso and Siobhan seek out the characters to use as patsies in their staged murder.

#### Encounters {#rthw:borca:adventuresinborca:lethallavender:encounters}

The adventure consists of these encounters.

**_Dig Your Vibe._** Eroso and Siobhan Manduchi approach the characters and invite them to go carousing if the adventurers agree to share tales of their exploits. The two are [**Friendly**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#friendlyattitude) and flattering.

**_Exquisite Corpse._** Soon after the characters leave the siblings, they hear a scream. If they investigate, they find Eroso’s stabbed corpse surrounded by guards. Siobhan accuses the characters of killing her brother. A group of four [**Guards**](#ref:monster:5195065-guard "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195065-guard") and one [**Guard Captain**](#ref:monster:5195064-guard-captain "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195064-guard-captain") (in the Manduchis’ employ) moves to kill the characters, while Siobhan vanishes into the night.

**_Wanted._** More guards hunt for the characters but are easy to avoid. Before the characters flee the community, Nostalia Romaine (Medium, Lawful Evil [**Assassin**](#ref:monster:5194904-assassin "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5194904-assassin")), who appears to be human, discovers them. She explains that she works for the Boritsi family, shares the details in “Situation” above, and insists the characters help her find the Manduchis.

**_Dead Walking._** Any character who spends a day searching for the Manduchis and succeeds on a DC 14 Wisdom ([**Insight**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) or Charisma ([**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check learns Eroso’s or Siobhan’s whereabouts. If they investigate, they find a public site—like a theater or bar—where the noble is behaving recklessly. These aren’t the actual Manduchis, but [**Waxworks**](#ref:monster:6491401-waxwork "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491401-waxwork") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#waxwork)) striving to destroy themselves in public. The characters encounter at least two of these creatures, perhaps more at your discretion.

**_Flawed Perfection._** After the characters encounter at least two self-destructive Manduchi waxworks, they find a **Waxwork** convinced that it is the new, improved Eroso Manduchi, and it doesn’t want to die. If the characters promise to let it go, the Construct leads them to Lethal Lavender, the secret club where the Manduchis are hiding.

**_Lethal Lavender._** The interior of the Lethal Lavender club is overgrown with bruise-colored fungus. A pair of fungus-covered [**Zombie Plague Spreaders**](#ref:monster:6491406-zombie-plague-spreader "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6491406-zombie-plague-spreader") (see [chapter 5](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/rthw/denizens-of-the-mists#zombieplaguespreader)) haunts the bar. In the VIP lounge, the plant the Manduchis stole has bloomed into a [**Violet Fungus Necrohulk**](#ref:monster:5195253-violet-fungus-necrohulk "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195253-violet-fungus-necrohulk") composed of the real Manduchis and their remaining wax duplicates.

**_Ivana’s Favor._** After the monster’s defeat, Nostalia finds the characters. She rewards them with a vial of Boritsi perfume worth 2,000 GP and a letter from Ivana Boritsi inviting them to a private social event at Misericordia in a week’s time. Each character also earns a bonus 1,100 XP.
