# Introduction {#vtmbbb:introduction:Introduction}

## A World of Darkness {#vtmbbb:introduction:AWorldofDarkness}

You are already intimately familiar with our world. The sun rises, the sun sets, and, in between, people go about the business of living. Most of them never look too closely at what moves in the dark. The World of Darkness is a mirror of our world, our history, our cities. The horror of what you see in this mirror lives in the margins of the familiar.

Here, vampires have walked among humanity since before recorded history, moving through every age and empire, every era of plague and plenty. They are not a modern invention but an ancient entity, a continuous secret underpinning civilization as a whole.

That secret sounds rather different depending on the century.

In the Dark Ages, the world was lit only by fire. Most of Europe was forest. Old Roman roads cut through it in straight lines that represented a distant civilization’s expression of confidence. Outside the cities, the landscape is largely unmapped and ungoverned, full of things that have not yet been named and are certainly not waiting to be. It is a world of faith and superstition, of feudal courts and a wilderness that starts twenty yards past the last hut in the hamlet and does not end in any particular location.

This is the world of _Vampire: The Dark Ages_, and the setting of the included adventure “[One Last Goodbye](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye").” It is a fundamentally different world for vampires to inhabit than the modern nights their descendants will eventually learn to navigate. The political machinery that defines later vampiric society gets built here. Right now, it is still very raw.

In the modern era, vampires maintain what they call the Masquerade: a centuries-old tradition of concealment, a collective agreement to stay invisible to a mortal population that would destroy them if it truly believed. Vampires in the modern world exist inside that agreement, are shaped by it, constrained by it.

The Masquerade does not exist yet in the Dark Ages. Its foundation is still being built. Vampires have not yet collectively codified how they will interface with mortal civilization. Every peasant already believes in demons. The monstrous man in the castle who has not aged since the lord’s father was a boy is, to the village below, a basic fact of local life. The stakes of discovery are real, but faith does the work that secrecy will later have to do, and faith is much less precise. A vampire lord can be exposed by a suspicious priest or a child who saw the wrong thing at the wrong hour. The game of concealment is messier, more immediate, and more personal.

## Vampire and D&D {#vtmbbb:introduction:VampireandDD}

Games like _Vampire_, set within the World of Darkness, are built on the Storyteller system, a ruleset designed around collaborative narrative and moral complexity. Combat is certainly present, but it is not the engine. This system does what its name promises.

D&D is a different kind of game. It often utilizes maps, miniatures, structured encounters, and tactical combat as parts of its core pleasures. Adventures are built around objectives pursued through dangerous terrain, confrontations that play out in initiative order, and a party that solves problems by moving through space together. Adaptations of the World of Darkness to other systems are not unprecedented. GURPS produced _World of Darkness_ supplements in the 1990s, and the lesson those adaptations demonstrated holds here: when you have a great setting, different systems simply open different doors into it.

What the 5E format offers a Kindred party is tactical weight. The dungeon is sometimes literally a dungeon. The rival is something you have to fight through a burning building to reach. The wilderness has teeth and the map ends before the danger does.

Nosferatu moving through tunnels beneath a dying city, carrying information three factions will kill to possess. Gangrel tracking rivals through wilderness, far past the last torch on the road. Ventrue navigating a court where the mortal prince is someone else's pawn and the negotiation was designed to fail. A coterie arriving at a remote village where something has been feeding, and strong indication that the locals have already decided it must be them.

**_Welcome to the Dark Ages._**

## Kindred Class {#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass}

_A Vampiric Survivor Empowered by Blood_

KindredKindred are an ancient line of vampires who have existed since the first days of humanity. These vampires are innately social creatures who form their own socio-political sects and divisions, often based on clans and bloodlines. They gather for camaraderie and safety among the only beings who can understand the eternal hunger within.

Kindred draw their abilities, collectively known as vampiric Disciplines, from the supernatural power in their blood, which they more eloquently call vitae. The older and more powerful Kindred become, the more blood they can consume and concentrate within themselves, calling upon it to fuel their undead bodies and powers. These Disciplines allow them to do unbelievable things. They can possess intense strength, durability, or speed. They can transform into creatures of the night, or even mist. They can leash the wills of others. They can manipulate minds and perceptions. And they can reshape the darkness in which they are at home. Kindred can call upon truly terrifying powers, and they are all powered by vitae.

Kindred can develop in different ways, but as the centuries of their existence have proven, the clans and bloodlines carry specific aspects of their vampirism throughout the generations, from sire to childe. These manifest as each clan’s ancient bane, the cost to be paid beyond just the endless hunger for blood and the struggle to keep at bay the Beast inside. There are Kindred in every walk of life, it seems, hiding in plain sight, acting like mortals, and maintaining their eternal Masquerade.

#### Level 1: Feed {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level1Feed}

You feed on the blood of other creatures. This is most often accomplished through your sharp fangs. As an action, you can feed on another creature within 5 feet of yourself if the target is willing, [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) by you, or has the [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition), [**Grappled**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#grappledcondition), [**Paralyzed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#paralyzedcondition), [**Restrained**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#restrainedcondition), [**Stunned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#stunnedcondition), or [**Unconscious**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unconsciouscondition) condition. You can feed only once per turn.

When you feed, roll up to your maximum Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier (minimum of 1). The creature takes Necrotic damage equal to the roll’s total. This damage only reduces the creature’s Hit Point maximum by an amount equal to the roll’s total. The number of Feed Dice increases as you gain Kindred levels, as shown in the Feed Dice column of the [Kindred Features table](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeatures "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeatures"). If you roll a 6 on a d6 on a Feed Die, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. If a creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced to 0, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point.

**_Feeding from the Willing._** If you feed on a willing creature or a creature [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) by you, it can spend 1 Hit Point Die. It rolls the Hit Point Die and reduces its Hit Point maximum by the amount rolled, and you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. Feeding in this way doesn’t end the Charmed condition.

**_Hit Die Deficit._** If a creature has no remaining Hit Point Dice, the creature takes Necrotic damage equal to a roll of your Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier, and the creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the roll’s total.

**_Maximum Successful Feedings._** A creature can provide a number of Blood Points based on its size. A Small creature provides 1 Blood Point, a Medium provides 3, a Large provides 5, a Huge provides 7, and a Gargantuan provides 10. A creature regains all lost Blood Points when it finishes a Long Rest.

**_Creature Susceptible to Feed._** You can feed on any creature with blood or other life-giving fluid. Aberrations, Beasts, Dragons, Fey, Giants, Monstrosities, and Humanoids are the most common targets. Constructs, Elementals, Oozes, and Plants usually don’t have blood. Work with your GM to determine the types of creatures you can feed on.

> #### The Blood Bond {#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredclass:kindredclassfeatures:TheBloodBond}
>
> A mysterious power in the blood of Kindred causes creatures to become Blood Bound to that Kindred when:
>
> - They drink from the same Kindred at least three times within one year.
> - At least one Long Rest occurs between each of the three drinks.
>
> While Blood Bound to a Kindred, a creature is considered [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) by that Kindred and has [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on saving throws to avoid being Charmed or [**Frightened**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#frightenedcondition) by other creatures. This is in addition to the effects from the [**Kindred Vitae**](#ref:magic_item:11796006-kindred-vitae "https://www.dndbeyond.com/magic-items/11796006-kindred-vitae") consumable (see “[New Equipment](#vtmbbb:character-options:NewEquipment "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#NewEquipment")”).
>
> A Blood Bond can be suppressed for 1 minute if the creature succeeds on a DC 25 Charisma saving throw. The bond is only permanently removed if the creature doesn’t drink from that Kindred again for a year, or if the Kindred in question is killed.

#### Level 1: The Beast {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level1TheBeast}

You have a terrible Beast within that can only be sated with blood. As a Bonus Action, you can use this Beast’s predatory instincts to sharpen your senses and abilities. Until the start of your next turn, you have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on [**D20 Tests**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test) you make.

You can use this feature a number of time equal to your Proficiency Bonus, and you regain all expended uses when you finish a Short or Long Rest.

**_Hunger._** If a [**D20 Test**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test) benefiting from your Beast feature fails, you go into a bloodlusted, hunger-driven state. This bloodlusted state ends after 1 minute or after you have successfully used your [Feed](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurefeed "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurefeed") feature, whichever comes first.

While bloodlusted, you regard the creature nearest to you that you can see or hear and that you can feed on as your prey. If there are multiple possible creatures, choose one at random. On each of your turns, you must move as close to the creature as possible and take the [**Attack**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#attackaction) action to make an [**Unarmed Strike**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike) to grapple the target. If you’re unable to get close enough to the creature to grapple it, your turn ends after you’ve used up all your available movement. If the creature dies or can no longer be seen or heard by you, the next nearest creature that you can see or hear and that you can feed on becomes your new target. If the target is in range and has the [**Grappled**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#grappledcondition) condition at the start of your turn, you use your action to feed on it.

#### Level 1: Blood Potency {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level1BloodPotency}

You are fueled by blood, represented by Blood Points. Your Kindred level determines the number of points you have, as shown in the Blood Points column of the [Kindred Features table](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeatures "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeatures"). You can expend Blood Points in the following ways:

**_Heal Wounds._** As a Bonus Action, you can expend 1 Blood Point to regain Hit Points equal to 1d10 plus your Kindred level.

**_Hunger Sated._** If you fail a [**D20 Test**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test) that is affected by your [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature, you can expend 1 Blood Point to ignore the effects of Hunger.

**_Vitae Powers._** There are [Kindred feats](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeats "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeats") and [subclass](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredSubclasses "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredSubclasses") features fueled by expending Blood Points.

#### Level 1: Kindred Biology {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level1KindredBiology}

You are a specific kind of vampire known as a Kindred, which brings physical benefits and drawbacks.

**_Awaken._** You don’t regain expended Blood Points when you finish a Short or Long Rest. To gain the benefits of a Long Rest, you must have at least 1 Blood Point in your pool or you only gain the benefits of a Short Rest.

**_Darkvision._** You have [**Darkvision**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#darkvision) with a range of 60 feet.

**_Sanguivore._** You can’t eat food or drink. If you consume more than a nibble or sip, you have the [**Poisoned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#poisonedcondition) condition that ends once you expel what you consumed. While Poisoned in this way, you must succeed on a Constitution saving throw every 10 minutes to avoid having the [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition) condition, which lasts for 1 minute as you vomit up what you’ve consumed. The initial DC of this saving throw is 10, and it increases by 5 with each successive save.

Instead, you must consume a number of Blood Points per day equal to the number of pounds of food a creature your size normally requires per day (usually, 1 per day for Small and Medium creatures). You can expend 1 Blood Point to automatically succeed on the DC 10 Constitution saving throw to avoid gaining 1 level of [**Exhaustion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#exhaustioncondition) from [**Malnutrition**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#malnutritionhazard).

Expending Blood Points to activate your Kindred features or [Kindred Feats](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeats "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeats") does not count towards the Blood Points you need to expend for sustenance.

**_Sunlight Vulnerability._** You take 5 Radiant damage if you start your turn in sunlight.

**_Supernatural Spellcasting._** Charisma is your spellcasting ability for spells from your Kindred subclass and Kindred feats, your spells require no Verbal or Somatic components, and they require no Material components unless they are consumed by the spell or have a cost specified in it, taking damage can’t break your [**Concentration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#concentration).

**_Torpor._** If your [**Exhaustion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#exhaustioncondition) level is 6, you aren’t killed. Instead, you drop to 1 Hit Point, become [**Unconscious**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unconsciouscondition), and can’t remove the condition until you have expended a number of Blood Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus over any length of time. Another creature can feed you while you are Unconscious to recover Blood Points but the details of how that occurs is left for you and your GM to determine.

**_Undead._** You have the Undead (Kindred) creature type in addition to your normal creature type. You don’t age.

**_Vampiric Resilience._** You automatically succeed on Death Saving Throws. Being reduced to 0 Hit Points by Fire or Radiant damage, or being beheaded kills you outright.

**_Vampiric Vulnerabilities._** You have Vulnerability to Fire and Radiant damage.

**_Wooden Stake Through the Heart._** When you are attacked with a wooden weapon that deals Piercing damage and you are reduced to 0 Hit Points or the attack roll is a [**Critical Hit**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#criticalhit), you are staked through the heart and have the [**Paralyzed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#paralyzedcondition) condition until the weapon is removed.

#### Level 2: Kindred Feat {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level2KindredFeat}

You gain a [Kindred feat](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeats "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeats") of your choice for which you qualify. You gain this feature again at Kindred levels 7, 10, 13, and 17.

#### Level 3: Kindred Subclass {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3KindredSubclass}

You gain a [Kindred subclass](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredSubclasses "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredSubclasses") of your choice. A subclass is a specialization that grants you features at certain Kindred levels. For the rest of your career, you gain each of your subclass’s features that are of your Kindred level or lower.

> #### Creating Kindred Characters in the World of Darkness {#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredclass:kindredclassfeatures:CreatingKindredCharactersintheWorldofDarkness}
>
> In the _Vampire: The Masquerade_, Kindred are usually aware of their clan or bloodline soon after they are sired. In order to maintain game balance with other classes, levels 1 and 2 of the Kindred class are designed to represent the learning curve of becoming Kindred, a process that can take longer for some.
>
> In a campaign set wholly in the _World of Darkness_, it is suggested that Kindred characters be created at level 3. This allows them to choose their clan or bloodline from the beginning of the game, and this may allow them to create lengthier backstories that can bring their _Vampire: The Masquerade_ characters to life, proverbially speaking.

#### Level 4: Ability Score Improvement {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level4AbilityScoreImprovement}

You gain the [Ability Score Improvement](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/br-2024/feats#abilityscoreimprovement) feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify. You gain this feature again at Kindred levels 8, 12, and 16.

#### Level 5: Bestial Fury {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level5BestialFury}

Once per turn when you hit a target with a Melee weapon or [**Unarmed Strike**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike), you can expend a use of your [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature to roll the weapon’s damage dice twice and use either roll against the target. If this damage reduces it to 0 Hit Points, you can’t choose to leave the creature at 1 Hit Point as per the “[**Knocking Out a Creature**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#knockingoutacreature)” rule.

**_Bestial Flurry._** While affected by your Beast feature, you can attack twice instead of once whenever you take the [**Attack**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#attackaction) action on your turn.

#### Level 5: Improved Feed {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level5ImprovedFeed}

You can use the [Feed](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurefeed "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurefeed") feature as a Bonus Action. In addition, a creature [**Grappled**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#grappledcondition) by you has [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on its checks to escape the grapple.

#### Level 10: Vampiric Will {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level10VampiricWill}

You have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on saving throws to avoid or end the [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) condition, and against effects that turn undead.

#### Level 14: Efficient Feeding {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level14EfficientFeeding}

Whenever you roll a die for your [Feed](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurefeed "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurefeed") feature, you can reroll the die, and you must use the new roll.

#### Level 19: Epic Boon {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level19EpicBoon}

You gain an Epic Boon feat or another feat of your choice for which you qualify.

#### Level 20: Ancient Awakening {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level20AncientAwakening}

The essence of the original vampiric Antediluvians has awoken in you. Pick four Ability Scores. Increase each chosen Ability Score by 2, to a maximum of 25.

If a Kindred subclass feature allows for an Ability Score to be increased beyond 25, Ancient Awakening can increase that Ability Score up to the maximum noted in that other feature.

> Kindred or Cainite?
>
> In the Dark Ages setting, vampires do not yet call themselves Kindred. That term belongs to a later era, born from the political institutions that will eventually organize vampiric society across the modern nights. In the Dark Ages, vampires more commonly call themselves Cainites, tracing their lineage to Caine, the first of their kind.
>
> The Kindred class is designed to work across any era in which vampires have existed. In modern nights, “Cainite” carries specific weight as the preferred term of the Sabbat sect, and using it as a catch-all can create confusion. Kindred is used here as more neutral term for the class.
>
> If you are playing in a Dark Ages campaign, feel free to have your character use Cainite at the table. The rules work the same either way. What you are, regardless of the century, is something that drinks blood and does not die easily.

## Kindred Subclasses {#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredSubclasses}

A Kindred subclass is a specialization that grants you features at certain Kindred levels, as specified in the subclass. These subclasses represent your vampiric clan or bloodline. This section includes six of the original thirteen: [Brujah](#vtmbbb:character-options:brujah "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#brujah"), [Gangrel](#vtmbbb:character-options:gangrel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#gangrel"), [Lasombra](#vtmbbb:character-options:lasombra "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#lasombra"), [Nosferatu](#vtmbbb:character-options:nosferatu "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#nosferatu"), [Toreador](#vtmbbb:character-options:toreador "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#toreador"), and [Ventrue](#vtmbbb:character-options:ventrue "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#ventrue").

**Note:** Kindred subclasses give you a subclass-specific Bane feature. This is a thematic weakness attached to that specific clan or bloodline.

### Brujah {#vtmbbb:character-options:Brujah}

_Stand Up, Stand Firm, and Fight for Something_

[![Clan Brujah Crest, a helmet with a flaming crown atop a shield bearing an hourglass image (Artist: Richard Thomas)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-002.brujah-crest.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-002.brujah-crest.png)

The Brujah are a clan of freethinkers and revolutionaries who thrive on their own liberty and fight against oppression and stagnation. They find a power in themselves that is reflected as strength, speed, and a force of personality that makes them formidable in the varied conflicts they often find themselves in.

#### Level 3: Brujah Bane {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3BrujahBane}

Your blood simmers with the rage of your vampiric ancestors. If you fail a [**D20 Test**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test) that is affected by your [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature and choose to expend Blood Points to ignore the effects of Hunger, you must expend a number of Blood Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus to ignore the effects, instead of 1.

#### Level 3: Fighting Fury {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3FightingFury}

You can channel the rage simmering below the surface, funneling it into your physical strength and speed. You can expend 1 Blood Point to gain the following benefits for 1 minute.

**_Brawn._** You have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on the first attack roll you make with a Melee weapon or [**Unarmed Strike**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike) on your turn.

**_Swiftness._** You can take the [**Dash**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#dashaction) or [**Dodge**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#dodgeaction) action as a Bonus Action.

**_Weapon Training._** You gain proficiency with Martial weapons, and your training with weapons allows you to use the mastery properties of two kinds of Simple or Martial Melee weapons of your choice.

#### Level 6: Fearsome {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level6Fearsome}

Your vampiric presence exudes menace from the rise of your inner Beast.

**_Dread Gaze._** You bare your fangs, your eyes flash with predatory hunger, and you let out a hiss that strikes terror in the hearts of others. You can expend 1 Blood Point to cast the [**Fear**](#ref:spell:2618872-fear "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618872-fear") spell.

**_Predatory Aura._** You have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on Charisma ([**Intimidation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) checks.

#### Level 9: Fleetness {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level9Fleetness}

Your celerity grants you bursts of incredible speed.

**_Dizzying Speed._** You can expend 3 Blood Points to cast the [**Haste**](#ref:spell:2619141-haste "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619141-haste") spell on yourself. When you cast the spell in this way, when the spell ends, you aren’t [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition) and your Speed isn’t reduced to 0.

Color Illustration (Artist: Kieran Yanner)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-003.brujah-kindred.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-003.brujah-kindred.png)

#### Level 11: Burning Wrath {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level11BurningWrath}

You pour all of your intensity and fury into yourself, causing your blood to seemingly boil on your skin. As a Bonus Action, you can expend 2 Blood Points to empower your [**Unarmed Strikes**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike). For 1 minute or until you fail to make an attack roll against an enemy on your turn, whichever comes first, you can deal an extra 2d8 Necrotic damage to a target you hit with an Unarmed Strike.

#### Level 15: Rouse Emotions {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level15RouseEmotions}

You can extend your own passionate feelings into others, infecting them with your fervor. You can expend 3 Blood Points to cast one of the following spells

**_Enthrall._** You cast the [**Charm Person**](#ref:spell:2618964-charm-person "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618964-charm-person") spell as if with a level 6 spell slot, and the creatures have [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on the saving throw.

**_Incite._** You cast the [**Confusion**](#ref:spell:2619043-confusion "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619043-confusion") spell. A creature that fails its saving throw automatically rolls 7–8 to determine its behavior for each turn.

**_Terrorize._** You cast the [**Fear**](#ref:spell:2618872-fear "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618872-fear") spell. When you cast the spell in this way, you can modify it so that it doesn’t require [**Concentration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#concentration) and creatures have [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on the saving throw.

#### Level 18: Puissant Might {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level18PuissantMight}

Your raw, supernatural strength is legendary.

**_Ability Score Increase._** Your Strength score increases by 4, to a maximum of 30.

**_Pulverize._** When you hit a target with an attack roll using a Melee weapon or an [**Unarmed Strike**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike), your Melee weapon or Unarmed Strike deals two extra dice of its damage.

### Gangrel {#vtmbbb:character-options:Gangrel}

_Let the Beast Inside in Order to Control It_

[![Clan Gangrel Crest, Wolves holding a shield bearing a wolf head image (Artist: Richard Thomas)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-004.gangrel-crest.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-004.gangrel-crest.png)

The Gangrel have made peace with their Beast long ago, choosing to harness the wild power it promises and wield it rather than trying to stifle it. They are attuned to its feral nature, embodying the predator.

#### Level 3: Gangrel Bane {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3GangrelBane}

The Beast reveals itself in you as animalistic features and behaviors. If you are affected by the Hunger of your [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature, you have [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) and [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) checks until you finish a Long Rest.

#### Level 3: Gifts of the Beast {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3GiftsoftheBeast}

Your body reflects the protean gifts of your animalistic Beast. You gain the following benefits.

**_Eyes of the Beast._** The range of your [**Darkvision**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#darkvision) increases to 240 feet.

**_Feral Weapons._** As a Bonus Action, you can expend 1 Blood Point to grow rending claws for 1 minute. Your [**Unarmed Strikes**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike) deal 2d6 Slashing damage, and you can use your Dexterity modifier instead of your Strength modifier for the attack and damage rolls of your Unarmed Strikes.

#### Level 6: Feral Spirit {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level6FeralSpirit}

Your connection to your wild self forges a spiritual bridge with Beasts.

**_Beastmind._** While you look in the eyes of a Beast within 30 feet of yourself, you can speak telepathically with it, and you can use any of the [**Influence**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#influenceaction) action’s skill options with it.

**_Like Minds._** You can cast the [**Animal Friendship**](#ref:spell:2618847-animal-friendship "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618847-animal-friendship") spell without expending a spell slot. And when you cast the spell in this way, it is cast as if you had used a spell slot level equal to your Proficiency Bonus.

**_Wild Hunger._** When you are affected by the Hunger of your [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature, you can deal 1d6 extra Slashing damage when you hit it with an [**Unarmed Strike**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unarmedstrike), and you also count as one size larger when determining what you can [**grapple**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#grappling).

#### Level 9: Gifts of Survival {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level9GiftsofSurvival}

Your body is built to survive whatever the world sets before you.

**_Feral Fortitude._** When you take damage except Fire and Radiant, you can take a Reaction to reduce the damage taken by your Proficiency Bonus plus your Constitution modifier (minimum reduction of 1).

**_Meld with Earth._** As a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action, you can expend 1 Blood Point to cast the [**Meld Into Stone**](#ref:spell:2619031-meld-into-stone "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619031-meld-into-stone") spell, except the surface must be dirt, sand, or clay instead of stone. Additionally, while the spell is active, you gain [**Tremorsense**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#tremorsense) with a range of 60 feet. The spell lasts until you end it (no action required) or when you finish a Long Rest.

[![Clan Gangrel Warrior side on. wearing dark shirt and trousers. with a dueling gove and sword. Color Illustration (Artist: Kieran Yanner)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-005.gangrel-kindred.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/01-005.gangrel-kindred.png)

#### Level 11: Corporeal Mastery {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level11CorporealMastery}

You embody the supernatural powers of ancient vampire legends, transforming for a time.

**_Beast Form_**. As a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action, you can expend a number of Blood Points to [**shape-shift**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#shapeshifting) into a Beast form applicable to this feature (see “Known Forms” below). You stay in that form for a number of hours equal to half your Kindred level or until you use Corporeal Mastery again, have the [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition) condition, or die. You can also leave the form early as a Bonus Action.

**_Known Forms._** You can expend 2 Blood Points to shape-shift into a [**Bat**](#ref:monster:4775803-bat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775803-bat"), [**Cat**](#ref:monster:4775808-cat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775808-cat"), [**Rat**](#ref:monster:4775836-rat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775836-rat"), [**Raven**](#ref:monster:4775837-raven "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775837-raven"), or [**Wolf**](#ref:monster:4775850-wolf "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775850-wolf"). You can expend 4 Blood Points to shape-shift into a [**Dire Wolf**](#ref:monster:4775812-dire-wolf "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775812-dire-wolf"), [**Giant Bat**](#ref:monster:5195012-giant-bat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195012-giant-bat"), [**Giant Rat**](#ref:monster:5195025-giant-rat "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/5195025-giant-rat"), or [**Panther**](#ref:monster:4775832-panther "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/4775832-panther").

**_Temporary Hit Points._** When you assume a Beast form, you gain a number of [**Temporary Hit Points**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#temporaryhitpoints) equal to your Kindred level.

**_Game Statistics._** Your game statistics are replaced by the Beast’s stat block, but you retain your creature type; Hit Points; Hit Point Dice; Intelligence, Wisdom, and Charisma scores; class features; languages; and feats. You also retain your skill and saving throw proficiencies and use your Proficiency Bonus for them, in addition to gaining the proficiencies of the creature. If a skill or saving throw modifier in the Beast’s stat block is higher than yours, use the one in the stat block. While in Beast form, you retain your personality and memories, and you can understand languages you know, but you can’t speak.

**_Objects._** Your ability to handle objects is determined by the form’s limbs rather than your own. In addition, your equipment merges into your new form. Nonmagical equipment that merges with the form has no effect while you’re in that form.

#### Level 15: Command the Beast {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level15CommandtheBeast}

You can impose your will upon wild things, allowing your Beast to overwhelm theirs.

**_Rein._** You cast the [**Dominate Beast**](#ref:spell:2619146-dominate-beast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619146-dominate-beast") spell as its level 7 version, without expending a spell slot, and you can modify it so that it doesn’t require [**Concentration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#concentration).

Once you cast the spell with this feature, you can’t do so in this way again until you finish a Long Rest.

**_Rouse._** When an Undead (Kindred) you can see within 30 feet of you is about to make a [**D20 Test**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test), you can take a Reaction and expend 1 Blood Point to force that creature to use its [Beast](#vtmbbb:character-options:level1featurethebeast "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#level1featurethebeast") feature on that D20 Test. If the Kindred has no remaining uses of its Beast feature, this effect automatically fails.

#### Level 18: Protean Rewards {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level18ProteanRewards}

Until the start of your next turn, you have a +3 bonus to AC, your Speed is doubled, your movement doesn’t provoke [**Opportunity Attacks**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#opportunityattack), and you have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on attack rolls.

**_Clairvoyance._** You can expend 2 Blood Points to cast the [**Clairvoyance**](#ref:spell:2618972-clairvoyance "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618972-clairvoyance") or [**Scrying**](#ref:spell:2619007-scrying "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619007-scrying") spell. When you cast the spell in this way, the sensor can’t be perceived.

**_Open Mind._** You can use a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action to expend 1 Blood point to choose any number of creatures you know within 5 miles of yourself or any number of creatures you can see within 120 feet of yourself. You and the chosen creatures can communicate telepathically with each other while the creatures and you are within 5 miles of each other for 8 hours. You and the targets can instantly share words, images, sounds, and other sensory messages with each other through the link, and the targets recognize you as the creature they are communicating with. This effect enables creatures to understand the meaning of your words and any sensory messages you send to them.

**_Star Magnetism._** You can expend 3 Blood Points to cast the [**Mass Suggestion**](#ref:spell:2619029-mass-suggestion "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619029-mass-suggestion") spell. When you cast the spell in this way, you can target any creature you can communicate with, you don’t need line of sight, the range becomes 1 mile, and you can modify it so that it doesn’t require [**Concentration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#concentration).

### Ventrue {#vtmbbb:character-options:Ventrue}

_Control Others with Influence and Guile_

**_Do as I Say._** You can cast the [**Command**](#ref:spell:2618985-command "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618985-command") and [**Suggestion**](#ref:spell:2619101-suggestion "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619101-suggestion") spells. #### Level 3: Ventrue Bane {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level3VentrueBane}

#### Level 6: Dare Not Falter {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level6DareNotFalter}

You effortlessly shrug off harm to your body and mind. You gain the following benefits.

**_Toughened._** Your Hit Point maximum increases by an amount equal to your Kindred level when you gain this feature. Whenever you gain a Kindred level thereafter, your Hit Point maximum increases by an additional 1 Hit Point.

**_Unswayable Mind._** If you fail a saving throw to avoid or end the [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition), [**Frightened**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#frightenedcondition), or [**Stunned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#stunnedcondition) condition, you can reroll it. You must use the new roll.

#### Level 9: Domineering Control {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level9DomineeringControl}

With a glance, you can steer the emotions and thoughts of intelligent creatures.

**_Befuddle._** You can expend 1 Blood Point to cast the [**Hypnotic Pattern**](#ref:spell:2619168-hypnotic-pattern "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619168-hypnotic-pattern") spell. When you cast the spell in this way, a creature with an Intelligence of 3 or lower automatically succeeds on its saving throw against the spell while a creature with an Intelligence of 4 or higher has [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on the saving throw.

**_Entrance._** As a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action, make a Charisma ([**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check and expend 2 Blood Points to choose a creature you can see within 30 feet of you. The target must make a Wisdom saving throw with a DC equal to the result of your Persuasion check. On a failed save, the creature has the [**Stunned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#stunnedcondition) condition until the end of your next turn. At the end of each of the Stunned creature’s turns, the creature repeats the save, ending the effect on itself on a success. A creature with an Intelligence of 3 or lower automatically succeeds on its saving throw against the effect.

**_Persuade._** You can expend 1 Blood Point to cast the [**Charm Monster**](#ref:spell:2618963-charm-monster "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618963-charm-monster") spell.

**_Terrify._** As a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action, make a Charisma ([**Intimidation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check and expend 1 Blood Point to choose a creature you can see within 30 feet of you. The target must make a Wisdom saving throw with a DC equal to the result of your Intimidation check. On a failed save, the creature has the [**Frightened**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#frightenedcondition) condition for 1 minute. At the end of each of the Frightened creature’s turns, the creature repeats the save, ending the effect on itself on a success. A creature with an Intelligence of 3 or lower automatically succeeds on its saving throw against the effect.

#### Level 11: Mass Manipulation {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level11MassManipulation}

Your ability to command and sway others grows to encompass multiple minds at once. You can expend 3 Blood Points to cast the [**Mass Suggestion**](#ref:spell:2619029-mass-suggestion "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619029-mass-suggestion") spell. At level 15, you can expend 4 Blood Points to cast the spell as its level 7 version; at level 18, you can expend 5 Blood Points to cast it as its level 8 version; and at level 20, you can expend 6 Blood Points to cast it as its level 9 version.

#### Level 15: Sanguine Fortification {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level15SanguineFortification}

Your vitae has resilience-enhancing properties. You gain the following benefits.

**_Draught of Endurance._** When a creature within 60 feet of you drinks your vitae, you can take a Reaction and expend 2 Blood Points to grant that creature a number of [**Temporary Hit Points**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#temporaryhitpoints) equal to twice your Kindred level.

**_Flesh of Marble._** When you take damage except Fire and Radiant, you can take a Reaction and expend 2 Blood Points to reduce the damage you take by half (round down) or 4 Blood Points to reduce the damage to 0.

#### Level 18: Lordly Power {#vtmbbb:character-options:Level18LordlyPower}

Your will over others becomes ironclad and staggeringly powerful. You gain the following benefits.

**_Imposing Aura._** When a creature within 15 feet of you targets you with an attack roll or a damaging spell, it must succeed on a Wisdom saving throw (DC 8 plus your Strength modifier and Proficiency Bonus) or either choose a new target or lose the attack or spell. On a successful save, the target isn’t affected, and it’s immune to Imposing Aura for 24 hours.

**_Penetrating Dominance._** Creatures you can see within 60 feet of yourself have [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on saving throws to avoid or end the [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) condition.

**_Summon._** As a [**Magic**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#magicaction) action, you can expend 3 Blood Points to urge a Beast, Humanoid, or Undead you have touched and is on the same plane of existence as you to make a Wisdom saving throw (DC 8 plus your Charisma modifier and Proficiency Bonus). On a failed save, the creature is compelled to come to you. The creature must use as much of its movement as possible to move in your direction on its turns, taking the safest route, and stopping only to eat and rest. A compelled creature repeats the save at the end of each day at dusk, ending the effect on itself on a success. You know if the compulsion ends early. You can’t have more than one creature under this effect at a time. If you use this feature again, the effect on another creature ends.

When the creature appears, it is under no compulsion to behave a particular way. You can ask it to perform a service in exchange for payment, but it isn’t obliged to do so. The requested task could range from simple (fly us across the chasm, or help us fight a battle) to complex (spy on our enemies, or protect us during our foray into the dungeon). You must be able to communicate with the creature to bargain for its services. Payment can take a variety of forms.

The GM can adjust these payments based on the circumstances under which you called upon it. If the task is aligned with the creature’s ethos, the payment might be halved or even waived. Nonhazardous tasks typically require only half the payment, while especially dangerous tasks might require a greater gift. Creatures rarely accept tasks that seem suicidal.

A [**Remove Curse**](#ref:spell:2618943-remove-curse "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618943-remove-curse"), [**Greater Restoration**](#ref:spell:2618961-greater-restoration "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2618961-greater-restoration"), or [**Wish**](#ref:spell:2619213-wish "https://www.dndbeyond.com/spells/2619213-wish") spell ends this effect.

## Feats {#vtmbbb:character-options:Feats}

The following feats are designed for the [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass") or classes adventuring in the World of Darkness.

### Origin Feats {#vtmbbb:character-options:OriginFeats}

These feats are in the Origin category.

#### Healthy {#vtmbbb:character-options:Healthy}

_Origin Feat_

You have, for whatever reason, maintained a physically healthy lifestyle. You gain the following benefits.

**_Hit Die Use._** The first time you would spend a Hit Point Die after completing a Short or Long Rest, you gain the benefit of spending the Hit Die but do not spend it.

**_Saving Throw Proficiency._** Choose one ability in which you lack saving throw proficiency. You gain saving throw proficiency with the chosen ability.

#### Nocturnal {#vtmbbb:character-options:Nocturnal}

_Origin Feat_

You have spent many formative years in the darkness of night. You gain the following benefits.

**_Darkvision._** You have [**Darkvision**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#darkvision) with a range of 60 feet. If you already have Darkvision, its range increases by 30 feet.

**_Slink in Shadows._** Whenever you make a Dexterity ([**Stealth**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check while in [**Dim Light**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#dimlight) or [**Darkness**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#darkness), you can treat a roll of 9 or lower on the d20 as a 10.

#### Protected {#vtmbbb:character-options:Protected}

_Origin Feat_

You have someone or something guarding you for as long as you can remember, even if you don’t know why. You gain the following benefits.

**_Luck Points._** You have a number of Luck Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus and can spend the points on the benefits below. You regain your expended Luck Points when you finish a Long Rest.

**_Reroll._** Whenever you make a [**D20 Test**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#d20test) and roll 9 or lower on the d20, you can spend 1 Luck Point to reroll the d20. You must accept the second result.

**_Stabilize._** When you are reduced to 0 Hit Points and not killed outright, you can spend 1 Luck Point to drop to 1 Hit Point instead.

#### Thin-Blooded {#vtmbbb:character-options:ThinBlooded}

_Origin Feat (Can Be Chosen in Place of Any Origin Feat)_

You have a portion of a vampire’s blood flowing in your veins. You are a thin-blood, often destined for a place among the clanless. You gain the following benefits.

**_Able to Feed._** You feed on the blood of other creatures. This is most often accomplished through your sharp fangs. As an action, you can feed on another creature within 5 feet of yourself if the target is willing, [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) by you, or has the [**Incapacitated**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#incapacitatedcondition), [**Grappled**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#grappledcondition), [**Paralyzed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#paralyzedcondition), [**Restrained**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#restrainedcondition), [**Stunned**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#stunnedcondition), or [**Unconscious**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#unconsciouscondition) condition. You can feed only once per turn.

You have 2d6 Feed Dice. When you feed, roll up to your maximum Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier (minimum of 1). The creature takes Necrotic damage equal to the roll’s total. This damage only reduces the creature’s Hit Point maximum by an amount equal to the roll’s total. If you roll a 6 on a d6 on a Feed Die, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. If a creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced to 0, you can regain 1 expended Blood Point.

**_Feeding from the Willing._** If you feed on a willing creature or a creature [**Charmed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) by you, it can spend 1 Hit Point Die. It rolls the Hit Point Die and reduces its Hit Point maximum by the amount rolled, and you can regain 1 expended Blood Point. Feeding in this way doesn’t end the Charmed condition.

**_Hit Die Deficit._** If a creature has no remaining Hit Point Dice, the creature takes Necrotic damage equal to a roll of your Feed Dice plus your Constitution modifier, and the creature’s Hit Point maximum is reduced by an amount equal to the roll’s total.

**_Maximum Successful Feedings._** A creature can provide a number of Blood Points based on its size. A Small creature provides 1 Blood Point, a Medium provides 3, a Large provides 5, a Huge provides 7, and a Gargantuan provides 10. A creature regains all lost Blood Points when it finishes a Long Rest.

**_Creature Susceptible to Feed._** You can feed on any creature with blood or other life-giving fluid. Work with your GM to determine the types of creatures you can feed on.

**_Kindred, but Not Kindred._** You ignore the Kindred class prerequisite for choosing [Kindred feats](#vtmbbb:character-options:kindredfeats "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#kindredfeats"). You can’t gain levels in the [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass").

**_Kindred Vitae._** You have a number of Blood Points equal to your Proficiency Bonus minus one.

**_Supernatural Spellcasting._** Charisma is your spellcasting ability for spells from your Kindred feats. Your spells require no Verbal or Somatic components, and they require no Material components unless they are consumed by the spell or have a cost specified in it. Taking damage can’t break your [**Concentration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#concentration) for spells cast using this feature.

**_Vampiric Vulnerabilities._** You have Vulnerability to Fire and Radiant damage.

**_Wooden Stake Through the Heart._** When you are attacked with a wooden weapon that deals Piercing damage and you are reduced to 0 Hit Points or the attack roll is a [**Critical Hit**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#criticalhit), you are staked through the heart and have the [**Paralyzed**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#paralyzedcondition) condition until the weapon is removed.

#### Well-Read {#vtmbbb:character-options:WellRead}

_Origin Feat_

You grew up with your nose buried in books. You gain the following benefits.

**_Creature Lore._** When you roll Initiative, you can recall lore about a creature you can see within 30 feet of yourself; you know one Immunity, Resistance, Vulnerability, or Trait of that creature. You decide when you use this ability.

**_Font of Useful Knowledge._** When you roll a 1 on an Intelligence ([**Arcana**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**History**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check, you can reroll the die, and you must use the new roll.

**_Rare Knowledge._** If you succeed on an Intelligence ([**Arcana**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**History**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check, you learn additional lore. The GM has final say on what you learn, if anything. The lore might consist of important details, amusing revelations, or even secret lore that has never been widely known. The more information you already know about the thing, the more precise and detailed the information you receive is. That information is accurate but might be couched in figurative language or poetry, as determined by the GM.

### Kindred Feats {#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredFeats}

These feats belong to the Kindred category.

#### Alacrity {#vtmbbb:character-options:Alacrity}

_Kindred Feat (Prerequisite: Level 2+, Kindred Class)_

# One Last Goodbye {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:OneLastGoodbye}

A _Vampire: The Dark Ages_ Adventure Compatible with 5.5e Rules for Level 4 Characters by Bryan C.P. Steele

[![Close up of a hooded snarling vampire with a settlement in the distance behind them. Black and white illustration (Artist: Fred Harper)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-001.vampire-fangs.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-001.vampire-fangs.png)

There is a worldThere is a world hidden from humanity that survives in the space between superstition and disbelief. This shadowed world of secrets exists alongside the mortal world and has for tens of thousands of years. The denizens of this dark world prey upon humanity while desperately clinging to what was once mortal inside themselves.

It is a World of Darkness in which the monsters that go bump in the night are all too real, and you can become one of these supernatural creatures. You can become a Kindred, or, as they are more commonly known in folklore … vampires.

## Kindred and the Greater World of Darkness {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:KindredandtheGreaterWorldofDarkness}

Vampires exist. In modern times, they call themselves Kindred. They have survived in secret since biblical times, drinking blood to maintain their existence and sate the eternal hunger of the Beast that rages within.

In the Dark Ages, the time when this adventure is set, vampires more commonly call themselves Cainites, tracing their lineage to Caine, who was often cited as the first of their kind. However, there are varied legends of where and how the first vampire came into being, and surely the truth lies somewhere between them all.

As their name implies, Kindred draw their lineages back to a small assortment of original bloodlines from which they all stem. These ancient bloodlines, called clans by the Kindred, create a familial bond that helps shape each into something both unique and familiar to others who share that lineage.

Over millennia, the Kindred of each clan found others to share their immortal curse with. Many times, these new additions to the bloodlines were chosen for similar interests, beliefs, or personal connections, and no matter how far removed from their original vampiric roots, something in their blood remembered. With the addition of new generations of ageless Kindred created by the last, the clans progressed through time. They are powerful in the sense that they can perform wondrous and unexplainable feats of the supernatural, but with that power comes an endless and sinister price to pay—the Beast.

Each Kindred fights the eternal hunger for blood. At all times, the Beast claws within their motionless hearts, urging them to feral violence and the need to sate a thirst that often goes beyond the simple need for sustenance. The stronger the Beast becomes, the farther Kindred pull away from what made them human. As the years, decades, and even centuries pass, it becomes harder for Kindred to remember what it is like to be mortal, to see humans as relationships instead of nourishment. When Kindred lose their grip on what it is to be human, the Beast takes the reins, and a frenzy of bottled-up monstrosity erupts. Most forge mental and emotional anchors, called Touchstones, to keep them grounded in their humanity with the passage of time. It is the dichotomy of their curse—the need to exist alongside and within the realm of humanity, but to forever battle the urge to consume it.

Even with the vampiric powers lurking within their blood, Kindred learn early in their existence about their weaknesses. The merciless touch of fire and sunlight. The instant paralysis of wood through the heart. The entropic decline of going without feeding. These are real threats to a Kindred’s existence, but nothing can compare to their truest foe—the atrocities that spring from humanity’s uncaged fears. Humans fear what they don’t understand, and they often destroy what they fear. This fact, combined with their vast numbers, forces the Kindred to maintain a clandestine Masquerade to keep their existence a secret from the mortal world. They wear their figurative masks, walk amongst humanity, and do their best to avoid revealing their existence. If humanity ever really believed vampires were hidden among them, they would go to extreme lengths to wipe the Kindred out. This is why the Kindred set rules and traditions for themselves and those they create; what good is supernatural strength, telepathic dominance, or marble-hard flesh when hundreds of incensed and afraid mortals descend upon you while you sleep?

Where we find the Kindred, we find fractured souls trying to stave off the eventual fall to their Beast’s monstrous instincts, all while doing their best to be a part of the secret World of Darkness that envelopes their existence. The question all Kindred must ask themselves is: Can I indulge the monster within without letting it be what defines me?

### Terms Frequently Used in Vampire: The Masquerade {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TermsFrequentlyUsedinVampireTheMasquerade}

### Terms Frequently Used in _Vampire: The Masquerade_ {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TermsFrequentlyUsedinVampireTheMasquerade}

The following are specific terms used by Kindred in the _Vampire: The Masquerade_ setting. They might appear in this adventure or be used in dialogue.

**Beast, The.** The instinctual urges that allow a vampire’s hunger to unleash a ravening monster.

**Blood Bond.** A supernatural power that is held over an individual who has imbibed the vitae of a particular vampire three or more times.

**Cainite.** During the Dark Ages, vampires commonly referred to themselves using this term.

**Childe.** The term used to describe a vampire’s offspring made by turning a mortal. The plural of which is childer.

**Clan.** A group of vampires who share common characteristics passed through their vampiric bloodline.

**Coterie.** A small group of Kindred who assist, support, and understand each other, often through common interests.

**Discipline.** The collective term for vampiric abilities.

**Domain.** A territory over which a Kindred claims control.

**Embrace, The.** The process by which one Kindred turns a mortal into a Kindred. The Embrace requires a living creature to be drained of its blood and replaced with any amount of Kindred vitae.

**Final Death.** When a vampire is destroyed completely.

**Ghoul.** A mortal servant created by gifting vampiric blood to a living creature without draining its blood first (avoiding the Embrace).

**Haven.** The physical place a vampire slumbers during the day

**Hunger, The.** The all-encompassing urge to gorge blood that a vampire must constantly overcome.

**Kindred.** Vampires of the modern age typically refer to themselves using this term.

**Kine.** A clinical or vulgar term for humans who serve only as food.

**Lupine.** The vampire term for werewolves, their fearsome mortal enemies.

**Masquerade, The.** The traditional vampiric practice of hiding the existence of vampires, and other supernatural entities, from humanity, particularly by avoiding creating proof of anything supernatural.

**Retainer.** A mortal creature willingly or unknowingly used as a controlled resource by a Kindred.

**Sire.** The term used for the vampire who Embraced a mortal, gifting vampiric ascension.

**Thrall.** A creature placed in servitude of a Kindred, often through a Blood Bond or other supernatural coercion.

**Torpor.** A paralytic, deathlike state Kindred assume when they have suffered too much damage, gone without blood for too long, or both.

**Touchstone.** A person, place, or object that is meaningful to vampires and reminds them of their humanity. It is a major emotional event when a Touchstone is endangered or destroyed.

**Vitae.** The vampiric term for Kindred blood.

## Getting Started {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:GettingStarted}

To play through the events of the of _One Last Goodbye_, a few important things must be addressed:

- **The Storyteller.** The term “GM” typically refers to the person running the background and rules elements of the adventure. In _Vampire: The Masquerade_, this role is referred to as “The Storyteller” to help players see the game as a narrative experience instead of a set of obstacles to overcome or interactions to be “gamed.” In this or any other product involved with the greater World of Darkness, any references to GM, Game Master, or Storyteller are interchangeable.
- **Kindred Needs.** It is highly encouraged for the Storyteller running a _Vampire: The Masquerade_ adventure to become familiar with the specific needs and requirements of [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass") characters, such as feeding rules, finding places to avoid daylight, and the mechanics of Blood Points, which are important aspects of Kindred existence.
- **The Masquerade.** Kindred live a hidden, dangerous existence in the _Vampire: The Masquerade_ setting. They survive in secret by avoiding encounters that could attract attention to themselves, using stealth, guile, and manipulation to keep their supernatural nature from being revealed. Kindred are quite formidable in a violent encounter; however, it is sometimes better for them to err on the side of maintaining the Masquerade.
- **Time Period.** Many _Vampire: The Masquerade_ and World of Darkness products take place in a modern era, but the timelessness of Kindred allows for older periods to be compelling backdrops. For example, _One Last Goodbye_ takes place in the Dark Ages, a time when superstition and folklore carry much more weight amongst humanity.
- **The Characters.** In _One Last Goodbye_, the adventure is based around a small coterie of Kindred who have known each other long enough to travel together across Britannia and Northern Europe. These are best represented by four to five characters with 4 levels in the [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass"). This allows every member of the coterie to choose their subclass and have the skills needed to experience the adventure as written.

## Additional Rules for One Last Goodbye {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:AdditionalRulesforOneLastGoodbye}

## Additional Rules for _One Last Goodbye_ {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:AdditionalRulesforOneLastGoodbye}

Apart from the Storyteller needing to fully understand how the [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass") functions and reading the adventure fully to understand what events may or may not transpire, there are two additional rules in effect for _One Last Goodbye_, detailed as follows.

### Masquerade Level {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:MasqueradeLevel}

It is easier for Kindred to survive amongst humanity when mortals don’t suspect there are monsters in their midst. As a _Vampire: The Masquerade_ adventure progresses, there are encounters and scenes that can be made more or less difficult based on the Masquerade Level, a measure of how wary the local populace is toward the possibility of monsters lurking in the night.

There are six Masquerade Levels (listed in ascending order): Superstition, Disbelief, Skepticism, Concern, Wariness, and Alarm.

1.  **Superstition.** Most people don’t believe in vampires or other monstrous creatures and dimiss such stories.
2.  **Disbelief.** A few people believe vampires exist, but they are ignored or thought ill.
3.  **Skepticism.** There are rampant rumors about strange happenings, but most events are dismissed with the right application of logic.
4.  **Concern.** Too many unexplained events and circumstances have everyone asking questions. People are beginning to investigate.
5.  **Wariness.** People know the night is dangerous, and they often lock themselves inside at dusk or form patrols.
6.  **Alarm.** Monsters are real, and humanity must hunt them!

Most adventures begin at the Superstition level. There are choices the characters can make that prompt the Storyteller to raise or lower the Masquerade Level. Apart from localized effects detailed in each section, some encounters are altered when the Masquerade Level reaches certain points. The use of Masquerade Level helps the Storyteller create a more realistic environmental response to Kindred characters who are making careless or foolish choices as the story progresses.

### Narrated Feeding {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NarratedFeeding}

Kindred must drink blood to survive; it’s that hunger that drives them and creates dramatic tension in the narrative. For [Kindred class](#vtmbbb:character-options:KindredClass "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#KindredClass") characters, feeding can be made part of—or the reason for—additional combat encounters. An adventure where one or more characters are Kindred will likely call for feeding outside of planned encounters. When playing _Vampire: The Masquerade_ using fifth edition rules, unnecessary combat can slow the game. To help alleviate this, the Storyteller can occasionally use these rules for Narrated Feeding.

Instead of requiring an entire combat encounter, Narrated Feeding allows the Storyteller to give characters the chance to perform their necessary feeding “off camera” with a single skill check. The skill a Kindred decides to use varies based on the style of feeding the character is attempting, and the DC of the check is based upon the environment in which the feeding attempt is being made, as shown on the table [below](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:narratedfeedingtable "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#narratedfeedingtable").

**Hunting Grounds.** An area frequently hunted and maintained by numerous Kindred as part of a monitored domain.

**Urban.** A populated area within the confines of a town or city. A small village or town is considered Light, while a city or metropolis is Heavy.

**Rural.** A small community outside the confines of a population center, such as a farm or campground.

**Wilderness.** An area with few human inhabitants. This represents Kindred choosing to primarily feed on animals or wayward travelers.

The Storyteller can allow the Narrated Feeding check to be made with [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) if the Kindred has a feat or class feature that can modify the skill in question, or the check is made with [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) if the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") is too high (as determined by the GM) or other circumstances warrant it.

If a Narrated Feeding attempt succeeds, the Kindred can roll its Feed Dice to determine how many Blood Points it regains. No matter the result of the roll, the Kindred regains a minimum of 1 Blood Point.

If a Narrated Feeding attempt fails, the Kindred doesn’t gain a Blood Point and the Storyteller can decide if the situation warrants greater penalties, such as gaining 1 level of [**Exhaustion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#exhaustioncondition) or increasing the Masquerade Level.

## Our Story Begins … {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:OurStoryBegins}

_One Last Goodbye_ takes place in and around the small village of Knock Glad, in the northernmost part of Irish Britannia. It is late autumn, roughly 700 CE. The rains have grown cold, snow has started to dust the highlands, and the days are growing shorter with each dusk.

It is the Dark Ages, a time when humans feared predators more than they do in modern times. Without fear of camera phones or security systems to surveil them, Kindred can move and hunt with greater ease, but humanity’s stronger superstitions leave little room for mistakes that break the Masquerade. One Kindred’s lack of control, succumbing to the Beast just once, can bring hunters, inquisitors, werewolves, and mages rooting around for monsters to slay.

The characters are a coterie of Kindred who have known each other long enough to seek havens together when travelling and have protected one another on many occasions. Their current journey has brought them to Knock Glad after receiving terrible news. The last living descendant of one of the characters, a young woman in her late 20’s named Aislynn, has died. The people of Knock Glad are holding a four-day-long wake for Aislynn, and the coterie feels it is necessary to accompany their friend to the event. As one of the character’s [Touchstones](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade"), having a chance to give a proper farewell can be the difference between moving on or falling into a psychological spiral that leads to the Beast.

### The Threat {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TheThreat}

When the characters first arrive, the locals believe that Aislynn was the unfortunate victim of a brazen predator, such as a dire wolf or similar wild animal, that is now stalking the outskirts of town. They soon pick up on rumors suggesting a more unnatural creature may the cause, perhaps even a Kindred.

Ultimately, the characters discover that Aislynn was killed by a [**lupine**](#ref:monster:6631104-lupine "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631104-lupine"), a werewolf, a creature that most Kindred fear for good reason. While the Kindred control cities, lupines control the wild.

> Who Is Aislynn?
>
> Unless one of the players chooses to make Aislynn a descendant and [Touchstone](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade"), the Storyteller can randomly determine who she was related to. The Storyteller should encourage the player to help describe why Aislynn is important to their character. It is important for the story that the characters have an emotional attachment to Aislynn, as the circumstances surrounding her death are the central mystery of _One Last Goodbye_.

## Welcome to Knock Glad {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:WelcometoKnockGlad}

The [**Masquerade Level**](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") in Knock Glad at the beginning of this adventure is set to **Disbelief**.

The small town in northern Irish Britannia known as Knock Glad is actually a series of small villages close enough to one another to share a central lowland square comprised of public halls, smokehouses, a stone chapel, the local cemetery, and a few tented stalls trading local wares. With a collective population of a few hundred scattered around the area, Knock Glad rarely appears as a bustling community except for religious gatherings and public celebrations.

One such gathering is the elaborate wake leading up to the funeral of a beloved member of the community, Aislynn. For over half a week, the community cooks, feasts, plays music, and congregates each night in a public hall to tell stories and celebrate Aislynn’s life. The first day is a general welcoming of family and friends to inform the gathering of what happened. The second day is a more sorrowful collection of songs and stories referring to what the community will miss about Aislynn. The third day is joyous, filled with drink and memories celebrating her life. The fourth day sees the community come together to say goodbye and inter the physical remains while toasting to the future of Aislynn’s loved ones. It has been this way for generations.

The character related to Aislynn knows of these traditions and can give the group a summary of the typical happenings on each of these four nights.

The characters arrive in Knock Glad just a few hours after sundown when the first night’s festivities are fully underway.

> #### Masquerade Level in the Dark Ages {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:welcometoknockglad:MasqueradeLevelintheDarkAges}
>
> People in the era where _One Last Goodbye_ takes place are a far more superstitious lot, and most believe evil might lurk in the shadows, even if they aren’t specifically vampires.
>
> The [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") in most isolated settlements is set to Disbelief.

## Night One—Finding Hospitality {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightOneFindingHospitality}

[![Villagers looking warily toward you. Black and white illustration (Artist: Vince Locke)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-002.villagers.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-002.villagers.png)

The people of Knock Glad have come together to begin the wake of Aislynn Fearghus, a beloved seamstress, who was the last living relative of one of the characters. The largest public hall in the central square is well lit, packed with a few dozen patrons, and cookfire smoke curls from the stacked-stone chimney.

The characters arrive at the perfect time to investigate and socialize with the community and learn about the area, what happened to Aislynn, and where to make a haven for themselves in the 9 remaining hours before dawn.

When the characters enter the public hall, read the following aloud:

> The heavy wood doors part, inviting you inside a welcoming place that starkly contrasts the mist and darkness outside. Collective warmth from the burning hearth and townsfolk dancing, drinking, and laughing washes over you, as does a mélange of scents. Mutton. Fermented spirits. Sweat. And, of course, blood. The air is thick with it, the amassed aroma of life, the seductive scent leaves your extremities tingling and your gums aching.
>
> It is quite early in the night, and this gathering of souls, here to sing poor Aislynn to her eternal rest, is a perfect place to ingratiate yourself with the locals. Perhaps it will help make the next few nights transpire more easily, as it could be fertile ground to hunt for information as well as your next meal.

The characters can socialize with the people of Knock Glad, learning about them and the events that have transpired these last few weeks. There are more than two dozen celebrants in attendance, but four of them stand out as the centers of social attention:

- **Aengus Clieregh [EEN-guhs KLE-uhrig] (He/Him).** Town blacksmith and stablemaster; a thickly muscled, hard worker who had plans to court Aislynn.
- **Cellach Baer [KALL-ah BAIR] (He/Him).** Mortar-mixer and bricklayer; older man who will have a draught with anyone who needs someone who will listen or a shoulder to cry on.
- **Deirdre Chonaill [DEER-dre KON-nal] (She/Her).** Wool spinner and cloth weaver; Aislynn’s textiles partner and best friend, who has taken the loss quite badly.
- **Niamh Ó Conchobhair [NEEV AH KRAH-hur] (She/Her).** Spirits trader; her husband makes the finest drinks in the highlands, and she brings them to trade.

While some information is gossip or alcohol-loosened rumors, the characters can find leads and lore. Each character can choose to make one of the following checks to learn useful information.

- **Deception.** A successful DC 14 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check misdirects the locals and deceives them into saying more than they intend.
- **Insight.** A successful DC 14 Wisdom ([**Insight**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check watches and listens for townsfolk to reveal details in moments of emotional weakness.
- **Investigation.** A successful DC 14 Intelligence ([**Investigation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check picks out important details from the clamor around the character.
- **Persuasion.** A successful DC 14 Charisma ([**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check convinces mourners to open up and divulge what has been going on.
- **Stealth.** A successful DC 14 Dexterity ([**Stealth**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check allows the character to go unnoticed and eavesdrop on nearby conversations.

Failure has no direct negative effect, but failure might tempt a character to use Kindred abilities, which always risk revealing a Kindred’s nature. Each successful check grants the character the following pieces of information (in order):

- **Aislynn’s Death.** “That poor girl. I told her not to go out after dark. It was only a matter of time before the nightstalker moved on from sheep. A damned shame.”
- **Finding a Haven.** “Ol’ Kiernan’s place, the trapper’s lodge at the edge of Roilan’s green. I heard he hasn’t been able to get a single lodger since the ‘stalker has come. All those rooms, not a soul in ‘em. It’ll be a shame if he has to shutter up and head south.”
- **Recent Events.** “There have been a few new faces around the Glad these days. Aislynn was beloved far and wide, it seems, to have so many strangers come around to bid the lass farewell.”
- **Character Connection.** “Aislynn had family out’country, and I hear tell runners were sent to fetch them. I do hope they’ll make it before she is set to rest. Right now, she waits in the chapel for these days to pass.”

**_Filling in the Details._** Over the next few hours, the Storyteller can add more conversations and details about life around Knock Glad, but the most important details for the characters are covered above.

Collectively, the townsfolk believe the nightstalker is likely some kind of highland predator that has grown brazen and now comes into town to hunt. Some of the people (particularly those later at night, or who have drunk too much) claim that the nightstalker is a fae thing from the wild places, a beast that walks on two legs, and that all of the sheep it has killed had their throats torn apart.

**_Developments._** The gathered information might lead the characters to believe that a rogue Kindred is targeting Knock Glad, and poor Aislynn was hunted, which might send them seeking her remains to determine the cause of death. Should they choose to go this evening, the chapel has several (too many) [**Villager Militia**](#ref:monster:6631108-villager-militia "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631108-villager-militia") watching her remains while the “barber from three counties over” is headed to prepare her for tomorrow. The characters will have better access to the chapel on the following night when everyone else is at the public hall again.

### After Midnight {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:AfterMidnight}

Shortly after midnight, the townsfolk start heading home in small groups for safety, leaving the characters a few more hours to find somewhere to sleep through the day. If they listened to the rumors, there are two effective options: claiming an empty home or renting rooms in the trapper’s lodge.

**_Empty Homes._** There are several empty homes at the edge of town. They can be claimed by the characters, but it will take 1d6 hours or a successful DC 15 Intelligence check with Carpenter’s Tools to sunproof the glassless windows, peat-and-pitch thatched roof, and stacked stone walls. This is the easiest way to create a temporary haven in Knock Glad, but wary characters might not desire to be in a simple structure around so many mortals. In addition, people seeing movement in a home where the locals know no one lives can attract attention (and could increase the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") by one step). If the Kindred choose this option, they can spend the rest of the night finding and preparing this haven, likely heading directly to [Night Two—The Chapel](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightTwoTheChapel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#NightTwoTheChapel").

**_Trapper’s Lodge._** The trapper’s lodge right outside of town has several rooms for rent. There are rooms without external access and doors that can be locked, making them perfect as havens. The characters must walk to the lodge as soon as they can, hoping to catch the trapper before he closes for the night. Proceed to “[Anyone Home?](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:anyonehome "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#anyonehome")” below.

#### Midnight Snack? {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:MidnightSnack}

With the public hall emptying and everyone heading home in small groups of three to four [**Villagers**](#ref:monster:6631107-villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631107-villager") (see [Appendix A](#vtmbbb:appendix-a-bestiary:Villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/appendix-a-bestiary#Villager")), feeding is more difficult. If the characters choose to use [Narrated Feeding](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:narratedfeeding "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#narratedfeeding") on their first night in Knock Glad, the feeding check DC increases by 4.

### Anyone Home? {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:AnyoneHome}

The characters have ventured just outside of Knock Glad, following directions or intuited pathways to the local trappers’ lodge. Domnall Raigh, seasoned wilderness survivalist and local trapping mentor, built this large, six-room lodge with the hope he would one day have a big family with many sons to carry on his trade. Five years ago, his wife, Uasal, died from “wet lungs,” and he turned his home into a place traveling huntsmen and trappers can hold up in for a few coins or a share of their hunt.

As the characters approach the front of the Domnall’s lodge, read the following aloud.

> The swollen moon, nearly full, backlights a lodge atop a hill. There is a light layer of fresh frost on the ground, and the stiff grass crunches underfoot. There are flickers of light, likely from peat burners or tallow candles, glowing through the few windows of the sturdy wooden walls. A sliver of dancing light calls your eyes to the front door, which is hanging open. A few steps closer, and the breeze picks up, dragging the unexpected scent of fresh blood.

**_Traces of Blood._** A character with a Passive Perception 15 or higher or that succeeds on a DC 15 Intelligence ([**Investigation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) or Wisdom ([**Survival**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check discovers splotches of fresh blood on the ground at the door. Scattered droplets and booted tracks lead into the stony highlands (and the “[Trapper Trapped](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:trappertrapped "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#trappertrapped")” encounter below). Should a character choose to taste the blood, it is from a human.

**_Inside the Building._** The lodge is deserted. The door is open and a few lights are left burning. The inside shows signs of a fierce struggle. There is blood on the floor, and a smeared handprint on the wall near a rack for a bow and quiver.

**_Searching the Lodge._** Anyone taking the time to search the lodge finds a wooden lockbox (a successful DC 16 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check or DC 10 Dexterity ([**Sleight of Hand**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check with Thieves’ Tools opens the lock). It contains a rolled-up piece of vellum with a faded painting of a young woman, 11 silver coins, and a simple but elegant ring made from polished copper (worth 2 GP). In addition, there is a variety of hunting tools, wilderness gear, and all the normal things you would believe a professional trapper would have in a home.

#### Resting for the Night {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:RestingfortheNight}

If the characters choose to stay in the lodge for the night, lock the door, and shutter the windows, they can; Domnall will be overtaken and killed by the wolves described in the “[Trapper Trapped](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:trappertrapped "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#trappertrapped")” encounter. However, the townsfolk will find his remains the next day, blame the nightstalker, and the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") will increase by one.

#### Following the Tracks {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:FollowingtheTracks}

If the characters investigate, following the tracks is easy. Domnall and the wolves aren’t trying to hide. It takes 20 minutes for the character to catch up. Proceed to the “Trapper Trapped” encounter.

### Trapper Trapped {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TrapperTrapped}

The trail leads into the stony highland, right to the mouth of a cave that is a den for highland wolves.

As the characters approach the clearing around the cave, read the following aloud:

> The last swath of sap-coated branches parts to reveal a moonlit clearing at the foot of a stony rise. In the wall of the rise looms the dark split of a cavern entrance, but that seems nowhere as important as the scene around it. Back against the stones is a desperate man. He holds a broken bow like some kind of quivering sword, futility keeping a closing circle of shaggy grey wolves from closing. Blood runs freely from numerous bites on his arms and legs, and true fear has pulled the color from his cheeks.

[![Map: Highland Wolves’ Den View Gridless Version (Artist: Josh Orchard)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/map-02-001.highland-wolves-den.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/map-02-001.highland-wolves-den.jpg)

**_Creatures Present and Initiative._** The [**Trapper**](#ref:monster:6631106-trapper "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631106-trapper") (see [Appendix A](#vtmbbb:appendix-a-bestiary:Trapper "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/appendix-a-bestiary#Trapper")) Domnall is at 10 of his 39 Hit Points. He is surrounded by a number of [**Highland Wolves**](#ref:monster:6631103-highland-wolf "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631103-highland-wolf") equal to 2 plus the number of characters.

The characters have only moments to act. Each character can perform one action before rolling Initiative. Domnall’s Initiative is 3, but the Storyteller should roll normally for the wolves. During the scene, the following special rules are in effect.

- **Aggressive Creatures.** The Highland Wolves are more aggressive and fearless than normal. A successful DC 14 Intelligence ([**Insight**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) or DC 12 Wisdom ([**Animal Handling**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check reveals this, but it doesn’t give hints as to what caused it.
- **Domnall Retreats.** Domnall uses the [**Disengage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disengageaction) action on his first turn, withdrawing into the relative safety of the cave as soon as the wolves are distracted by the characters. At the Storyteller’s discretion, Domnall’s hidden position in the cave limits his ability to see the characters and their actions.
- **Injured Wolves.** A Highland Wolf that starts its turn with 5 or fewer Hit Points uses the [**Disengage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disengageaction) or [**Dash**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#dashaction) action to flee into the wilderness.

**_Aftermath._** Once the Highland Wolves have been dealt with, the characters must deal with Domnall. There are several possible outcomes.

**_Domnall Died._** If the trapper is killed for any reason, the characters can choose what to do with his remains before turning his lodge or the cave into their temporary haven. The cave bends in a way that naturally shelters the inside from the sun.

**_Domnall Saw Too Much._** Domnall might have been thankful for the rescue, if his rescuers weren’t monsters themselves. If he sees anything unexplainably supernatural during the encounter, he flees from them as soon as he can. If the characters attempt to explain away what he saw, a successful DC 20 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check assuages his concerns. Otherwise, the characters can [**charm**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#charmedcondition) him or use other such Kindred abilities.

Even if calmed, Domnall wants to tell the community what happened. This increases the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") by one step. A successful DC 20 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills), [**Intimidation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills), or [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check convinces him to remain silent. This DC is reduced to 10 if Domnall hid in the cave before any PC activated a Kindred ability. Otherwise, if the coterie fails to convince him and they let him live, his tales raise the Masquerade Level by two steps.

**_Domnall Saw Nothing Questionable._** Domnall thanks the characters, saying he owes them his life, and invites them to his lodge for as long as they like for free. Domnall can become a steady source of food at the lodge (if the characters are careful), possibly convincing him to become some form of [Retainer](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade") or—with some cleverness—a [Ghoul](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade"). Domnall is now a staunch ally. The [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") doesn’t change.

#### Domnall’s Story {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:DomnallsStory}

If they give Domnall a chance to explain what happened, he tells them:

> “There I was,” Domnall sips from a beaten silver flask, “gettin’ ready to toss the innards of a couple of wee hares out the front, when two of those big bastards charged in on me. We’ve always had run-ins with the wolves out here, but not like this. They didn’t want the hares. They were after me. They had a look in their eyes, the devil it was, and they got a good clip out of me. I jabbed it with my porridge paddle; damn wolf snapped it in two and ran off. I should’a let ‘em go, but, but that’s when I heard it.”
>
> He shakes his head slowly before continuing.
>
> “T’was a man’s voice. Could’na hear what he was saying, but if a townie was in trouble, I wasn’t about to let these things have ‘em. So, I grabbed my bow, good lot it did me, and tracked ‘em to their den. Never found whoever was talkin’, so I hope they’re okay, or that I was just imaginin’ it. Either way, something wasn’t right with these wolves, and I’m forever in your debt.”

### Morning Approaches {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:MorningApproaches}

The characters can complete minor activities before the sun rises. After the sun rises, move to “Night Two—The Chapel.”

## Night Two—The Chapel {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightTwoTheChapel}

[![A chapel at night with headstones leading to its door. Black and white illustration (Artist: Vince Locke)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-007.graveyard-and-church.png)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-007.graveyard-and-church.png)

**_Events during the Day._** The town readies for the second night of Aislynn’s wake. This is a more solemn event, marked with sad tales, sorrowful memories, and general mourning over the community’s loss.

Unfortunately for the characters, two [**Monster Hunters**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") have come from the monastery in Cork to investigate the nightstalker. They arrived right before sunset and claimed the chapel as their residence. They aren’t allowing anyone in while they “inspect the poor lass for signs of the Adversary’s touch.” The Monster Hunters have come to stake the body of Aislynn and remove her head.

> #### Masquerade Level {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:nighttwothechapel:MasqueradeLevel}
>
> Unless changed by events in Day One, the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") on Day Two remains at Disbelief. Apart from specifically noted consequences during [Night One](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightOneFindingHospitality "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#NightOneFindingHospitality"), the Storyteller has the final say as to whether or not actions increase the Masquerade Level.

### Empty Streets? {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:EmptyStreets}

The characters notice the streets of Knock Glad are empty on the second night of the wake. It is not fear of the nightstalker that clears the streets but tradition. It is customary for only mourners to be out, and they are sequestered in the public hall. The other townsfolk stay in on this evening, usually drinking. On this night, it is better to be respectful of those who have something to say about Aislynn and her passing than partake in the wrong spirit of the occasion.

The collective emptiness of the town’s throughways has a few effects:

- **Feedings.** Narrative Feeding checks are made with [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) because of the lack of people in common areas. The Storyteller can lift this restriction if the character is clever.
- **Stealth.** Characters have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on Dexterity ([**Stealth**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) checks as long as they move without a light source. The lack of lanterns or torches from passersby leaves only the waxing gibbous moon to illuminate the open areas.
- **Observation.** Characters have [**Advantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#advantage) on Wisdom ([**Perception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) that rely on hearing because of the quiet streets. The Storyteller can choose to remove this bonus if the characters are around particularly noisy areas or people.

As the characters move around Knock Glad, they notice only two buildings (aside from the occasional home) show activity: the public hall and the chapel.

### The Public Hall {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:ThePublicHall}

The characters find a much more solemn and sorrowful collection of mourners. Folk are telling childhood stories or recent tales about how Aislynn touched their lives. They also reiterate how unfortunate it was that she never married or had children. It is a drastic break from the warm, welcoming celebration of the previous night.

#### Character Touchstone {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:CharacterTouchstone}

If the character for whom Aislynn was a [Touchstone](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade") attends the public hall, ask them how their character feels and their emotional attachment to the deceased. If the character stays, the Kindred must make a DC 15 Wisdom saving throw. On a successful save, composure is maintained, and the character navigates the scene normally. On a failed save, the character is overcome with the loss. This can be reflected in becoming emotionless, a surge of anger that leads to splintering a mug, or a deep sorrow that leads to shedding and quickly wiping away bloody tears before anyone notices. The player should decide what is a fitting and thematic with prompts from the Storyteller, but the character has [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) on Charisma checks that don’t involve [**Intimidation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) until finishing a Long Rest. The Storyteller is strongly encouraged to reward the player with [**Heroic Inspiration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#heroicinspiration) for good roleplay.

#### Conversations {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:Conversations}

Characters who listen to the conversation in the public hall hear the following.

- “Laoghaire’s two pups, the dogs, were found this mornin’. All tore up and bled out. Nasty work. The nightstalker for sure. I’m glad those lads from Cork are here. They’ll sniff it out. If not, I’ll not be leavin’ home without a dirk and a fork for sure.”
- “The monks from Cork should be here instead of holding up in the chapel. I know they said they had to make sure poor Aislynn is ready for the grave properly. Whatever that means, anyway. Still seems a wee disrespectful to me.”

The characters are informed, in one way or another, that the only other location worth investigating tonight is the chapel.

### Outside the Chapel {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:OutsidetheChapel}

When the characters arrive at the tamped gravel lot around the chapel, they see bright oil lanterns creating a constant glow behind the building’s shuttered windows. Silhouettes moving within reveal activity.

**_Sounds._** A Passive Perception 15 or higher or succeeding on a DC 15 Wisdom ([**Perception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check overhears prayer from inside. Once hearing or being made aware of the prayer, a successful DC 14 Intelligence ([**Religion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check reveals it to be an old exorcism ritual.

**_Windows._** The windows are taller than shoulder height, and the shutters don’t have latches. A successful DC 8 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check can lift a character into a window. An accompanying successful DC 13 Dexterity ([**Stealth**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check allows a character to do so quietly.

**_Doors._** The front doors are barred from the inside by a length of rain-softened wood. A straight blade of any length can slip between the doors to lift the bar with a successful DC 12 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) or Dexterity ([**Sleight of Hand**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check, but the falling bar, unless silenced, alerts the two [**Monster Hunters**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") within. Alternatively, a successful DC 16 Strength ([**Athletics**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check or single attack that deals 12 or more Bludgeoning or Force damage bashes open the door.

#### Moving Forward {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:MovingForward}

Unless the characters choose to ignore what is happening inside the chapel (and proceed to [Night Three—Search Parties](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightThreeSearchParties "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#NightThreeSearchParties")), they stumble upon a grisly scene in Paying Respects.

### Paying Respects {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:PayingRespects}

When the characters enter the chapel, read the following aloud:

> “. . . Ad pacem Dei eam mittimus!” A tall man in a leather coat shouts passionately, bringing an iron hammer down on a shaft of wood jutting from the chest of a young woman’s body on the altar. In your ears, the impact is louder than the prayer.
>
> “Et ita erit.” A second man states more cooly, standing on the opposite side of the altar. A long, silver blade in his hand flashes, punctuating his words by slicing through the body’s neck with an unforgettable sound of a sharp edge meeting the wood beneath.
>
> “Aye,” a villager addresses you, holding up a peat lantern in your direction. “What are you doing here?”

[![Map: Knock Glad Chapel View Gridless Version (Artist: Josh Orchard)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/map-02-002.knock-glade-chapel.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/map-02-002.knock-glade-chapel.jpg)

Two [**Monster Hunters**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") and a number of [**Villagers**](#ref:monster:6631107-villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631107-villager") (see [Appendix A](#vtmbbb:appendix-a-bestiary "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/appendix-a-bestiary")) equal to the number of characters plus the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") have finished performing a ritual based on a Spanish exorcism rite, which required the Monster Hunters to drive a wooden stake into her heart before removing her head with a water-purified silver blade.

#### Monster Hunter Interactions {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:MonsterHunterInteractions}

Depending on the coterie’s initial reaction to the purification (or desecration, based on the point of view) of Aislynn’s body, the characters can use manipulation or misdirection to avoid a fight.

If the hunters have reason to believe the characters are anything but caring townsfolk who have come to pay respects, they attack. Each [**Monster Hunter**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") makes a DC 15 Intelligence ([**Religion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**Arcana**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check. On a success, a Monster Hunter notices something strange or supernatural about the characters, leading the hunters to think one of them is the nightstalker. A successful DC 25 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check dissuades the two Monster Hunters from attacking. Otherwise, they attack immediately.

#### Combat {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:Combat}

There are two [**Monster Hunters**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") and a number of [**Villagers**](#ref:monster:6631107-villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631107-villager") equal to the number of characters plus the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel"). The Villagers help the Monster Hunters.

Most folk are in their homes or at the public hall while this scene takes place, and those who have come around in the past hours have heard loud praying inside. Unless the combat is so wild that it burns the chapel down, it shouldn’t be interrupted.

#### Aislynn’s Remains {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:AislynnsRemains}

Unless the characters flee the scene during or after the encounter, they can investigate Aislynn’s remains. A successful DC 14 Intelligence ([**Medicine**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check reveals the following pieces of information:

- Aislynn has no blood. It was either drained during her death or after to preserve her for the wake.
- The salts and flowers used to keep the remains tolerable before interment have made it difficult to know exactly when she died.
- The [**wooden stake**](#ref:equipment:192-wooden-stake "https://www.dndbeyond.com/equipment/192-wooden-stake") in her chest and the beheading are exactly the wounds that would end a Kindred’s life. If Aislynn was in [Torpor](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade") and awaiting her first blood after a wrongful Embrace, she has met Final Death.
- There are jagged bite wounds on both sides of Aislynn’s neck, but they are too marred by the beheading to determine more information.

If the check fails, the wounds on the body are too extensive to determine anything useful.

Whoever this nightstalker is, it slew Aislynn with bites to the throat, and the hunters, with their ritual, ensured no other information is available.

> Silver Weapons?
>
> The [**Monster Hunters**](#ref:monster:6631105-monster-hunter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631105-monster-hunter") carry [silver weapons](#vtmbbb:character-options:silverweaponsandammunition "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/character-options#silverweaponsandammunition") because they have been taught that silver is the metal of purity and deals horrific wounds to monsters. The characters might laugh at the idea that silver is more effective than iron at harming Kindred, but the presence of such weapons foreshadows their use later in this adventure. The Storyteller may wish to highlight how rare and useful such weapons are in a world with real monsters. This foreshadowing shouldn’t spoil the true nature of the nightstalker.

### What to Do with All These Remains {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:WhattoDowithAllTheseRemains}

There may be the remains of the hunters and the villagers to deal with. Depending on what the chartacters choose to do with the bodies, the scene can influence the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel"). The Storyteller has final say, but creative ways to destroy or hide the bodies should be rewarded, while careless methods should have consequences. Here are examples:

- If the characters forget to do anything with the remains, leaving behind a brutal scene, the Masquerade Level is increased by one step. In addition, if the remains were quite obviously brutalized by fangs, claws, or supernatural strength, the Masquerade Level is increased by one additional step, for a total of two
- If the characters think to remove or hide the remains, a successful DC 14 Intelligence ([**Investigation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check reveals a shallow storage cellar under the back of the chapel. If the bodies are hidden there, the Masquerade Level doesn’t change.

If the characters remove the wooden stake from Aislynn’s body and rewrap her remains in linen to hide the results of the ritual, the Masquerade Level is reduced by one step (to a minimum of Disbelief).

Once the characters are done in the chapel, they have a few hours before sunrise if they wish to feed or perform other actions under the cover of night. The characters can retreat back to their haven to discuss events. Proceed to “Night Three—Search Parties.”

## Night Three—Search Parties {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightThreeSearchParties}

[![A street at night, a pair of women observe the malitia questioning people on the street. Black and white illustration (Artist: Martyna Zych)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-008.militia-questioning.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-008.militia-questioning.jpg)

The third night has a drastically different atmosphere than the previous night. The people take the celebration into the streets. While the public hall is prepared for the final toasts and well-wishing, groups of celebrants and mourners alike are encouraged to make merry. The town is filled with wandering parties who take their drinks and foodstuffs with them around the whole of Knock Glad. The streets are filled with telling and retelling stories about Aislynn.

> #### Masquerade Level {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:nightthreesearchparties:MasqueradeLevel}
>
> If the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") has reached Wariness level, each instance of one or more [**Villagers**](#ref:monster:6631107-villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631107-villager") always includes two [**Villager Militia**](#ref:monster:6631108-villager-militia "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631108-villager-militia") as well. If the Masquerade Level reaches Alarm, each encounter includes a [**Trapper**](#ref:monster:6631106-trapper "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631106-trapper") and the DC for [Narrative Feeding](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:narratedfeeding "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#narratedfeeding") increases by 5.

### Double-Edged Sword {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:DoubleEdgedSword}

The evening has groups of townsfolk addled by alcohol and exhaustion from three days of celebration. Interactions with these roving groups represent the predominant events of the evening, allowing the characters to choose how Night Three transpires.

Depending on the mentality of the characters, there are a number of ways to use the groups effectively. The following sections describe possible encounters.

#### Feeding {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:Feeding}

Each group is comprised of 2d4 [**Villagers**](#ref:monster:6631107-villager "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631107-villager"), each carrying torches. They roam about town, distracting themselves with drinks and stories.

#### Nightstalker Lure {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightstalkerLure}

If the characters suspect, either through the Storyteller’s descriptions of the wolves or by successful checks, that the wolves that attacked the trapper are supernaturally influenced, the characters might believe the nightstalker is a Kindred. And tonight makes for an ideal feeding opportunity, and an ideal opportunity to ambush a rogue Kindred. The characters can use their Kindred abilities and [**Stealth**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) to follow the townsfolk around, effectively using them as bait to lure Aislynn’s murderer into the open. Unfortunately for the characters, the nightstalker is going to be active well outside of town, a fact revealed too late in “[Lights at MacLaughlan’s Farm](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:lightsatmaclaughlansfarm "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#lightsatmaclaughlansfarm").”

#### Reinforcing the Masquerade {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:ReinforcingtheMasquerade}

If the coterie has been lax in upkeeping the Masquerade since arriving, the characters can use misleading conversation and convincing logical arguments to help start counter-rumors and gossip that can strengthen the Masquerade in the area. It takes three successful Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills) or [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) or Intelligence ([**Nature**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) checks over the course of the night to do so, but the characters can actually lower the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") by one step. The DC of this check equals 14 plus the Masquerade Level (Disbelief is DC 16, Skepticism is DC 17, Concern is DC 18, and so on). If the characters fail three checks, however, they inadvertently strengthen the rumors and increase the Masquerade Level by one step.

#### Socializing {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:Socializing}

The characters can choose to use some of the evening to ingratiate themselves with the townsfolk, listen to stories about Aislynn and generally partake in the wake’s festivities. This might not progress solving the nightstalker mystery, but it reinforces positive social connections. If the interactions are particularly pleasant, the Storyteller should give the characters [**Heroic Inspiration**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#heroicinspiration).

### Lights at MacLaughlan’s Farm {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:LightsatMacLaughlansFarm}

The interactions with (or avoidance) of the townsfolk throughout the night can affect the characters, but the most important moment of Night Three takes place within an hour of dawn. As the characters head toward the safety of their haven, they overhear a conversation from some stable hands just getting ready to start their day.

As soon as the characters say they are headed back to their haven, read the following aloud:

> “Whaddya mean there’s lights up at the MacLaughlan farm?” One voice was clear in the predawn quiet. “Old man MacLaughlan’s been dead for three weeks goin’, an’ his boy left last summer.”
>
> “Yeah, but I’m tellin’ ya,” a second, younger voice comments. “Eraign saw it and told me on her way to bed. Somebody is up at the MacLaughlan place. And it ain’t no MacLaughlan.”

Each character can make a DC 13 Wisdom ([**Insight**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check. The check is made with [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage) if the character doesn’t interact with the stable hands and only overhears them from a distance. On a successful check, the character discerns that the stable hands are honest, good folk. If the characters ask them further questions, they have the following pieces of information to give:

- “When the old man passed, Cellach sent for his only son, but the lad’s got family down south now. It’ll be a month or so before he can make the trip Glad’s way. We’ve been checkin’ in on the flock ‘til he gets in to claim it.”
- “The MacLaughlan farm is a windy road up into the berm. Takes an hour, maybe two, to head that way. Great grazing for the sheep, but it’d be a hike.”
- “The farm’s so far out that he keeps a full storage cellar for dairy and the like under the house. He had to, all that bobblin’ in the cart path would turn the cream to curds over the trip, for sure.”
- “When the son returns, I’m sure it’ll go to good use. I hope the lad brings his family here. The Glad could use some new blood, I say. Some fresh faces, like yours, perhaps.”

Should the characters attempt to head to the farm now, the Storyteller should remind them that it is right before dawn, and heading out is foolish. They can prepare after sundown, but they can’t safely do anything else before “Night Four—Vengeance for Aislynn. ”

## Night Four—Vengeance for Aislynn {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NightFourVengeanceforAislynn}

[![A wild eyed bearded man with long hair, holding a hand axe, stripped to the waist turns from a table upon which is a butchered sheep. Black and white illustration (Artist: Martyna Zych)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-009.butcher-lamb.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-009.butcher-lamb.jpg)

The final day of Aislynn’s wake starts with an unseasonably early snowfall drifting from the highlands. A pristine white carpet rests across Knock Glad and the surrounding lands. It isn’t a bewildering storm or a major hindrance, but many of the locals have taken it as a sign that Britannia itself is saying goodbye to Aislynn. The weather and the day’s planned events have pushed most people into the main public hall, eventually to the chapel, and then finally to the cemetery at dawn.

By the time the characters arise for the evening, most of the snow has been trodden to mud in the throughways, but the fields and paths surrounding the town are an unmarred stretch of white.

> #### Masquerade Level {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:nightfourvengeanceforaislynn:MasqueradeLevel}
>
> If the [Masquerade Level](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:masqueradelevel "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#masqueradelevel") has reached a level of Wariness or higher, the [Best Laid Traps](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:bestlaidtrapsoptionalencounter "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#bestlaidtrapsoptionalencounter") optional encounter should take place halfway through the trip to [The Nightstalker Lair](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:thenightstalkerlair "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#thenightstalkerlair").

### No Time to Waste {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:NoTimetoWaste}

As long as the characters didn’t ignore the conversation with the stable hands the night before, their priority should be the MacLaughlan farm.

When a character can see the fresh snow, the character must make a Wisdom ([**Perception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check. The highest result amongst the coterie is compared to the table below to receive the most detailed information about what they find.

**Fresh Snow Observations (Highest Perception)**

| Result      | Information Gained                                                                                                                              |
| ----------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Less than 5 | There might be tracks in the snow outside of town, but it is too muddy and trampled to be useful.                                               |
| 5–10        | Most tracks lead toward the public hall, but one set with a running stride leads out of town toward the direction of the MacLaughlan farmstead. |
| 11–15       | There are highland wolf tracks around and behind the human prints; they must have been chasing someone out of town.                             |
| 16+         | The wolf tracks aren’t chasing someone. The prints accompany the human tracks!                                                                  |

After discovering the tracks, the characters have a clear direction to head to find the nightstalker and avenge Aislynn.

The characters can visit the main public hall or chapel beforehand, finding that most folk are gathered in those two places to give their toasts and tell their final stories before her interment at sunrise. If the characters attempt to feed from these large groups, the check is made with [**Disadvantage**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/rules-glossary#disadvantage). If the character missed the information provided by the stable hands, they automatically learn it here.

Whether it is immediately after waking or after visiting the gathering places of the townsfolk, when the characters are ready to end this adventure, they can follow the tracks out of town. Proceed to “Best Laid Traps” or “[The Nightstalker Lair](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:thenightstalkerlair "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#thenightstalkerlair").”

### Best Laid Traps (Optional Encounter) {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:BestLaidTrapsOptionalEncounter}

Because of the rampant fear of the supernatural in the area, a [**Trapper**](#ref:monster:6631106-trapper "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631106-trapper") has set an ambush on the path between Knock Glad and the highlands. This devoted huntsman is hoping to catch and slay the nightstalker, but an untimely interaction with the characters might be what it takes for him to decide to put that label on them instead.

As the characters approach the halfway point between the town and the MacLaughlan farm, they have a chance to see the ambush before it is sprung. A character with a Passive Perception 16 or higher sees the snow-covered leather blind the trapper is hiding beneath and the iron traps he has strewn around the clearing. If one or more characters mention looking for trouble, a successful DC 16 Wisdom ([**Perception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check spots the ambush.

If the ambush goes unnoticed, the trapper waits until the characters are within 30 feet to attack, placing them in the midst of his Traps ability.

If the characters notice the ambush and call to the trapper, or if none of the characters are damaged during the first round of combat, they have a chance to defuse the situation. A single successful DC 14 Charisma ([**Deception**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills), [**Intimidation**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills), or [**Persuasion**](https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/free-rules/playing-the-game#skills)) check defuses the situation.

Whether the characters defeat or dissuade the trapper, proceed to “The Nightstalker Lair.”

### The Nightstalker Lair {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TheNightstalkerLair}

[![Tyrion, a wild eyed bearded man with long hair. Black and white illustration (Artist: Martyna Zych)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-010.tyrion.jpg)](https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/vtmbbb/FIPjWHwuc1dxehRt/02-010.tyrion.jpg)

The characters have reached the MacLaughlan farm where the nightstalker has made its home. The nightstalker is a [**Lupine**](#ref:monster:6631104-lupine "https://www.dndbeyond.com/monsters/6631104-lupine") (see [Appendix A](#vtmbbb:appendix-a-bestiary:Lupine "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/appendix-a-bestiary#Lupine")). Most werewolf tribes see the Kindred as eternal enemies. A single one of these monstrous shape-shifters is capable of slaughtering entire coteries. It is partly why Kindred avoid the unchecked wilderness. A single lupine running rampant, as is the case around Knock Glad, is something most vampires fear. A werewolf’s rage and capacity for violence is legendary.

This particular lupine came to the area along with a pack of highland wolves to prey upon the numerous sheep. By unlucky chance, it ran afoul of Aislynn and murdered her to remain hidden. After her untimely death, the lupine had planned to leave, but before it could, it sensed the arriving Kindred, the characters, and it stayed to sniff them out. While the characters were trying to find the nightstalker, it was trying to find them. Now those two opposed forces have come together for a violent clash at the MacLaughlan farm.

As the characters reach the farm, they see the paddock has numerous sheep, the windows are shuttered, and a splash of red can be seen on the snow at the paddock’s fence. There is also blood smeared on the handle of the front door.

When the characters enter the cottage, read the following aloud:

> The front door opens with the creak of iron hinges. The inside of the old cottage is warmed by a central hearth, but its tingle upon your undead skin is a sensation soon set aside by the scent of freshly spilled blood and the sound of a blade through gristle.
>

>
> Golden eyes shine angrily as a massive black wolf emerges from the back of the cottage, growling menacingly.
>
> “You should’ve ne’er come to this place.” The man’s eyes flare from sky blue to sunrise gold, and he grins. His mouth warps into a long muzzle full of ivory fangs while his body twists and expands into a nine-foot-tall pillar of lupine muscle and fur. “Now ye die!”

Either they will slay the beasts, or they will suffer a flesh-torn [Final Death](#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade "https://www.dndbeyond.com/sources/dnd/vtmbbb/one-last-goodbye#termsfrequentlyusedinvampirethemasquerade") like so many Kindred have at the claws and fangs of the Garou, the name the lupine call themselves.

### The Future of Knock Glad {#vtmbbb:one-last-goodbye:TheFutureofKnockGlad}

However events end at the MacLaughlan cottage, Knock Glad will never be the same. While the characters won’t be able to see it, Aislynn’s remains are laid to final rest in Knock Glad’s cemetery at sunrise so the rays of the sun are the last thing she “sees” before her eternal slumber. It is a beautiful ceremony that the characters won’t be able to witness, especially the one who is effectively saying goodbye to a piece of their mortal existence. The characters can stay in Knock Glad for as long as they wish, now that it is safe for them, turning Aislynn’s home into a new and wonderful haven for a long, long time.

The events of _One Last Goodbye_ leave Knock Glad changed in many ways, and how they fit into a larger role with the characters or the townsfolk may just be the beginning of a new story, a new adventure, or a memory to be recalled by the characters centuries later.
